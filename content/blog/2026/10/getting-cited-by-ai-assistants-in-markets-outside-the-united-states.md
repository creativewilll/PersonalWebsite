# Getting Cited by AI Assistants in Markets Outside the United States

**A citation outside the United States is your name, plus a URL, inside an answer that a person in that country can actually get.** The assistant has to be offered there. The page needs a URL that Google, or ChatGPT's search, can connect to that country. A rank you hold in the US is a different event.

How can my business get cited by AI assistants in markets outside the United States? I keep a market card. One country per card. I do not open a second country until the first card has a pass or a written miss.

I'm William Spurlock, founder of Spurlock Studios LLC. I work as an AI Systems Architect and Fractional AI CTO. The receipts I will stand on are 600+ automations built and 500+ live, 20,000+ hours architecting agentic systems, and 35,000+ hours saved for clients. I will not invent a foreign office, a local client, or a citation rate for a market I did not log.

The language problem, an English-only site dropping out when the prompt is in another language, is already written in [why an English-only site disappears in multilingual AI search](/blog/why-your-english-only-site-disappears-in-multilingual-ai-search). This post starts one step earlier. Is the assistant even on in that country, and which URL is the country object? Fan-out, the habit of running several related searches for one question, is covered in [how to rank when Google AI Mode turns search into a conversation](/blog/google-ai-mode-explained-how-to-rank-when-search-becomes-a-conversation). A mention on a site that already lives in the market is the third-party half, and that work sits with [what local news mentions do for AI visibility](/blog/what-local-news-mentions-do-for-ai-visibility).

I use Germany as the worksheet because I could see it on the lists I read on October 10, 2026. It is a worksheet. It is not a client.

## How can my business get cited by AI assistants in markets outside the United States?

**Confirm the assistant is offered in that country, publish one URL for that country, and get one page in that market to name you.** If any of those three is missing, you do not have a citation plan. You have a US site and a hope.

I will not start by buying a country-code domain. Google's own table says a country domain is a strong, clear signal, and also that it is expensive, sometimes restricted, and able to target only one country ([Google Search Central, last updated December 10, 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites)). A subdirectory on the domain you already run is the first URL I ship. The country domain can wait until that market is a real book of business.

The order is the whole method:

1. **Read the vendor's country list on the day you care.** Google's Search Help page and OpenAI's supported-country page are different lists. A country on one can be missing on the other.
2. **Pick the URL shape and stop.** Subdirectory first. I do not also launch a subdomain and a country domain in the same week.
3. **Put the country on the page.** Language, currency, and a sentence that names the country. Google lists those as audience signals.
4. **Earn one local mention.** A link from a site in that country is on Google's signal list. Your own homepage linking to itself is not that signal.
5. **Log the answer.** Name, URL, date, engine, prompt. A pass is your name and the country URL in the answer. Anything else is a note.

Google's Search Central guide, last updated December 10, 2025, says there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and no special schema you must add ([AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). Eligibility is still the ordinary bar: the page has to be indexed and allowed to show a snippet, and indexing is not guaranteed. The country problem is not about secret markup. It is about giving search a page it can connect to that country.

ChatGPT is a separate gate. OpenAI's search help, as I read it on October 10, 2026, says placement is not guaranteed, and that a site has to allow OAI-Searchbot if it wants to be eligible for ChatGPT search ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-chatgpt-search)). A German URL that blocks that crawler is invisible to that search, no matter how clean the copy is.

## What does a citation in a market outside the United States actually require?

**It requires an answer a person in that market can open, with your name and a URL that belongs to that market.** The assistant being switched on in the country is the first fact. It is not the citation.

I split those on purpose. Owners hear "AI Overviews launched worldwide" and hear "my US page is now the link in Berlin." Those are different sentences. Hema Budaraju's May 20, 2025 post says AI Overviews were then available in more than 200 countries and territories and in more than 40 languages, with Arabic, Chinese, Malay, Urdu, and more added ([Google, May 20, 2025](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/ai-overview-expansion-may-2025-update/)). The same post says that in the US and India, the feature was driving an increase of over 10% in Google usage for the query types that show AI Overviews. That is a usage figure. It is not a count of brands cited, and it is not a promise about your URL.

The list I actually check is newer than that announcement. On October 10, 2026, Google's Search Help page said AI Overviews are currently available to all users in a named set of countries, territories, and languages ([Google Search Help](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/14901683?hl=en)). On that page I saw Germany, Japan, India, Mexico, the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, and Ireland, among others. I am not going to publish a hand count of that table. The check is the page, read on the day you ship.

When I log a test, I write five fields and I leave the rest blank:

- **Date and engine.** October 10, 2026, and the product name. AI Overviews is not AI Mode. ChatGPT search is not the Gemini web app.
- **The prompt, in the market's language, with a city.** A prompt that never names the country is a US habit wearing a costume.
- **Whether the assistant was allowed to run there that day.** List on or list off. If it is off, I stop. I do not "test" a product the vendor will not serve.
- **The names in the answer, in order.** Your position matters. A mention in paragraph six is a different outcome from the first name.
- **The URL next to your name.** If the URL is the US page, the country card fails even if the name is right.

That is a language list for AI Mode. It is not the AI Overviews country table. I do not merge them on the card.

## Why doesn't a United States ranking carry into another country's AI answer?

**Because country availability, query language, and the URL Google treats as that country are three separate switches.** Winning the US English query closes none of them.

Google says it does not use IP analysis to adapt your content. IP location is difficult and generally unreliable, most but not all Google crawls originate from the US, and Google does not vary the crawl location to discover site variations ([Google Search Central, December 10, 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites)). If your only "country version" appears after you sniff the visitor's IP, Googlebot may never see it. I do not ship that pattern. I ship a URL that exists when a crawler from the US asks for it.

The same guide says Google ignores locational meta tags such as `geo.position`. A tag in the head that whispers "this page is for Germany" is not the signal. The URL, the language, the currency, and links from other local sites are.

ChatGPT handles location differently, and it still does not rescue a US page. The search help page I read on October 10, 2026 says ChatGPT may estimate a general location from the IP address, down to country, state, or city, and may share that general location with search providers. It does not share the IP itself, or the ChatGPT account, with those providers. Device location sharing is optional and off by default. If the area looks wrong, OpenAI's instruction is to put the city, neighborhood, or postal code in the question ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-chatgpt-search)). Naming Berlin in the prompt gives you a location to check against. A rank in Dallas is not.

Three mistakes I see owners make when a US rank feels like it should travel:

- **An IP redirect into a translated template.** Google's crawl note is the reason I refuse it. The buyer may land on a local page. The crawler may only ever index the US page.
- **A query-string locale, `site.com?loc=de`.** Google's URL table marks that pattern "Not recommended," because URL-based segmentation is difficult and users may not see the country in the address.
- **Treating English as one country.** Google's localized-pages example, on a doc last updated September 21, 2026, splits a generic English page, a UK page that shows pounds, and a US page that shows dollars ([localized versions](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions)). The United Kingdom, Australia, Canada, and Ireland were on the AI Overviews country list I read on October 10, 2026. An English page with US dollars is the wrong object for those buyers even when the words match.

The hreflang value itself is a language code in ISO 639-1, plus an optional region code in ISO 3166-1 Alpha 2. Google's doc says codes outside those lists, such as `es-419`, are not supported. Pairing rules, return links, and the `x-default` fallback are in the August post. I am not repeating that build here. I am telling you the region code is how an English page becomes a UK page instead of a second US page.

AI Overviews and AI Mode may also fan out, issuing several related searches for one question, and overviews often do not trigger at all ([Google Search Central, December 10, 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). One prompt about a German buyer can still pull a pricing page, a comparison, and a support doc. If the site only has a pricing page in dollars, that is the pricing page those searches can find. I want a euro URL indexed so those related searches can find a country page. I am not claiming Google promised to prefer it. I am claiming you cannot be the local link if you never published one.

Google also says some vanity country domains, such as .tv and .me, are treated as generic domains because people use them that way. A cute TLD is not a country strategy. And some countries restrict who can hold the real country domain. Google's line is to do that research first. I treat "we bought a domain we cannot legally use" as a worse outcome than a subdirectory.

The audience signals on the same page, the ones that are not the domain:

- **Local language and currency on the page.** For the Germany worksheet, German and euros. For the UK, English and pounds, which is Google's own example, not a translation project.
- **Links from other local sites.** One real mention beats a footer full of country flags. How those mentions behave in answers is the local-news post, not a second essay here.
- **Local addresses and phone numbers, and Business Profile where you have them.** Google lists these as signals. It also says "where available" for Business Profile. If you have no office in the country, you do not have this signal. I will not invent a street.
- **Server IP.** Google says it can be a hint and is not definitive, because CDNs and hosting countries break the assumption. I do not move a server to Frankfurt to win Germany.

Google's AI-features guide adds one operational line: keep Business Profile and Merchant Center information up to date, and make sure structured data matches the visible text. That is housekeeping. It is not a country substitute. The same guide says you do not need a special machine-readable AI file to be eligible.

What I refuse to count as a signal:

1. **A flag icon in the header.** It does not create a URL.
2. **A cookie that swaps the language in place.** Google wants explicit URLs. The August post covers why a translate widget is not a locale page.
3. **A locational meta tag.** Google says it ignores them.
4. **A second English homepage with the country name stuffed in the title and US dollars left in the body.** The region example exists so the prices match the country. The title alone is not the page.

My opinion is narrow. The country domain is the stronger geotargeting signal, and I still do not buy it first. I would rather have one subdirectory that states the currency, plus one local link, than a .de domain whose only page is the US homepage with a new coat of paint. Google's cons for the subdirectory are real. Users may not read "de" as Germany. That is why the page has to say Germany, in German, with euros, out loud.

## How do I check whether an AI assistant cited me in another country?

**I log a prompt that names the city, run by someone who is in that country, or I label a desk run as a language test and I do not call it a pass.** A VPN session is not a buyer.

OpenAI's search help says a VPN or a network location may change the approximate location inferred from the IP. That is a warning, not a method. You have moved the guess. You have not become a person who lives in Munich, with a German-language device and a history of German queries. Separately, OpenAI's supported-country page says that access from outside the published list may get the account blocked or suspended ([OpenAI Help, retrieved October 10, 2026](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7947663-chatgpt-supported-countries-and-territories)). A VPN that exits in an unsupported place can lock the tool you were trying to test. I do not use that as research.

The Gemini web app has its own list. The help page I read on October 10, 2026 says the Gemini web app is currently available in more than 70 languages and over 230 countries and territories, and that the Gemini mobile app may have different availability ([Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13575153?hl=en)). Checking the website list and assuming the phone matches is a bad check. I write down which app I opened.

Search Console will not close the card for you. Google says appearances in AI Overviews and AI Mode are included in the Performance report under the Web search type, mixed with the rest of web search ([AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). You can see clicks. You cannot read a row that says "cited in a German overview." I keep the card because the product does not keep it for me.

The check I actually run:

1. **Re-read the list.** AI Overviews help, OpenAI's supported countries, and the Gemini page for the app I care about. Write the date. Germany being present on October 10, 2026 does not mean I skip the read in March.
2. **Write the prompt in the market's language and include the city.** OpenAI's own fix for a wrong area is to put the city in the question. I do that even when I think the IP is right.
3. **Ask for the source list.** ChatGPT shows citations you can open. An overview shows links. If I cannot open the URL, I do not count it.
4. **Record the URL, not the vibe.** "It felt local" is not a row. The row is the address.
5. **Stop at one country.** A second country gets its own card. I do not average them into a "global visibility" score. That average hides the miss.

If the country URL is not indexed yet, the test is early. Google's guide is plain that meeting the technical bar does not mean the page will be crawled or served. I wait for the URL to be fetchable, then I log. I do not log a 404 and call the market hostile.

## FAQ

### Are Google AI Overviews available outside the United States?

**Yes, in the countries and languages on Google's current help list, which is wider than the US.** The May 20, 2025 announcement said more than 200 countries and territories and more than 40 languages. The help page I read on October 10, 2026 includes Germany, Japan, India, Mexico, the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, and Ireland, and it says the feature is available to all users in the listed places. I re-read that page before I promise a market. The 2025 post is the expansion. The help page is the list.

### Does ChatGPT work in every country?

**No. OpenAI publishes a supported-country list, and access from outside it may block or suspend the account.** I saw Germany on that list when I retrieved the page on October 10, 2026. I do not memorize the list, and I do not assume a country that is on Google's overview list is on OpenAI's. Search placement is a further bar: OpenAI says it is not guaranteed, and the site has to allow OAI-Searchbot.

### Does a VPN show the AI answer a buyer in that country sees?

**No. I do not brief a VPN screenshot as a local citation.** OpenAI says a VPN or network location may change the approximate location guessed from the IP, and that device location sharing is optional and off by default. A VPN moves a guess. It does not give you a buyer's language history. If the exit country is unsupported, you can also lose access to the account you were testing with.

### Should I buy a country-code domain first?

**Not as the first spend.** Google says a country domain is a strong signal to users and to search engines, and the same table lists the costs: money, infrastructure, rules about who can register, and a limit of one country. I ship a subdirectory, put the language and currency on it, and earn one local link. The country domain is a later decision, after that market is real. Vanity domains such as .tv and .me are treated as generic, so they are not a shortcut.

### Will Search Console show which country cited my page in an AI Overview?

**No. AI Overviews and AI Mode are counted inside the Web search type, with the rest of web search.** Google's December 10, 2025 guide says that plainly. You can see traffic. You cannot see a report that names the country whose overview cited you. The market card is the record. Search Console is the click record.

### Do I need a local street address to get cited outside the United States?

**No. An address is one signal, and only if it is real.** Google lists local addresses, phone numbers, and Business Profile where available, alongside local language, currency, and links from local sites. If you have no office in the country, you skip the address. You do not invent one. Language, currency, and a mention on a site in that market are the signals you can still earn.

### Is an English page enough for the United Kingdom or Australia?

**An English page is not a UK page or an Australian page.** Those countries were on the AI Overviews list I read on October 10, 2026, and Google's own example separates a UK page in pounds from a US page in dollars. Use a region code such as en-GB, which is an ISO 3166-1 Alpha 2 code on top of the language code. Leave the dollars page for the US. The August post is where the hreflang pairing rules live.

### Does the country where my server sits decide the market?

**No. Google says server location can be a hint and is not definitive.** CDNs and hosting in a country with better infrastructure break the shortcut. Google also says it will not vary the crawler by location to find hidden versions of a page. Put the country in a real URL. Do not put it in the data center and hope.

### Is the Gemini phone app available in the same countries as the Gemini website?

**Do not assume it.** The Gemini Apps Help page I read on October 10, 2026 says the web app is in more than 70 languages and over 230 countries and territories, and that the mobile app may have different availability. Write down which app you opened. A pass on the website is not a pass on the phone, and neither one means your page was the link.
