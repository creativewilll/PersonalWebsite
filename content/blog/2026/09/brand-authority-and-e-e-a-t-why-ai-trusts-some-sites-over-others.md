# Brand Authority and E-E-A-T: Why AI Trusts Some Sites Over Others

**Why does AI trust some sites over others?** It trusts the page where the byline, the claim, and a second source already agree. Google calls that pattern E-E-A-T. ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews do not keep an E-E-A-T scoreboard. They quote the page they can defend if a person checks the sentence.

I am William Spurlock, AI Systems Architect and Fractional AI CTO at Spurlock Studios LLC. I have spent 20,000+ hours architecting agentic systems, built 600+ automations with 500+ of them live, and that work has saved clients 35,000+ hours. Those receipts only help an answer engine when they sit on a page with my name, a date, and a way to see the work. A logo bar does not do that job.

This is the pillar for brand authority on this site. The person-record work lives in [how to build the author authority AI looks for](/blog/how-to-build-the-author-authority-ai-looks-for). The off-site mentions live in [digital PR for AI visibility](/blog/digital-pr-for-ai-visibility-getting-mentioned-where-ai-reads). The brand-as-a-thing work lives in [entity SEO](/blog/entity-seo-how-to-make-your-brand-something-ai-actually-knows). Here I am explaining what sits underneath those tactics: why one site gets quoted and the lookalike next to it does not.

As of September 2026, the public Google documents I use for this are still the Search Central pages last updated December 10, 2025, and the Search Quality Rater Guidelines dated September 11, 2025. If a vendor sells you an "E-E-A-T score," they invented the unit.

## What is E-E-A-T and why does it matter for AI visibility?

**E-E-A-T is Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness, and trust is the one that carries the other three.** On December 15, 2022, Elizabeth Tucker wrote on the Google Search Central blog that E-A-T gained an E for experience, and that the rater guidelines do not directly influence ranking. Google's creating-helpful-content page, last updated December 10, 2025, says the same thing in plainer language: E-E-A-T itself is not a specific ranking factor. Systems use a mix of factors to notice content that shows those qualities. Trust is most important. The other three contribute to trust, and a page does not have to show all of them to be useful.

That matters for AI visibility because answer engines inherit the same preference. A model that cites a scam page gets corrected in public. A model that cites a page with a name, a method, and a second source can point at the receipt. E-E-A-T is the human description of that preference. It is not a field you fill in on a form.

Google's own split, from that December 15, 2022 post, is the cleanest example I know. Tax-return instructions should come from someone with accounting expertise. A review of tax software can come from a person who used the product. Experience and expertise are different proofs. I use that split on every service page I touch. A roofing company does not need a professor byline. It needs the person who walked the roof, named, with the city and the year.

The September 11, 2025 rater guidelines put Trust at the center on purpose. The line I keep is blunt: untrustworthy pages have low E-E-A-T no matter how experienced, expert, or authoritative the creator seems. Their example is a financial scam run by an expert scammer. Skill does not launder the page.

What E-E-A-T is not:

- It is not a single ranking factor. Google says so on the helpful-content page updated December 10, 2025.
- It is not a score the raters type into the live algorithm. That same page says rater data is not used directly in ranking algorithms. Raters tell Google whether the systems look right. A restaurant does not let the comment card set the menu prices.
- It is not "about 16,000 people voting your URL up." Google's November 2023 rater overview says the company works with about 16,000 external Search Quality Raters. They rate sample results. They do not pick your ranking.
- It is not only a Google slogan. ChatGPT and Perplexity still have to attach a sentence to a URL they can stand behind. The letters are Google's. The behavior is any engine that cites.

YMYL is where the weight goes up. Google uses "Your Money or Your Life" for topics that can affect health, financial stability, safety, or the welfare of society. On those topics the helpful-content page says systems give even more weight to content that lines up with strong E-E-A-T. A local plumber's service area page and a medical-claims page are not the same bet. I still want a name on both. I want tighter sourcing on the one that can hurt someone.

The rater guidelines also tell raters to look at what other people say. Independent reviews, references, and news articles are the reputation check. A site that only talks about itself has a thin file. That is brand authority in one sentence: other pages, not you, are willing to repeat your name next to a fact.

## Why does AI trust some sites over others?

**An engine trusts a site when three records match: the page, the named person or company, and an independent source.** Miss one and the model has a reason to cite someone else. I call that corroboration. It is the whole game behind "why does AI trust some sites over others," and it is stricter than "we post a lot."

Google's AI features documentation, last updated December 10, 2025, describes the mechanical half. To show up as a supporting link in Google AI Overviews or AI Mode, a page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Google Search with a snippet. There are no extra technical requirements, and there is no special schema you must add for those features. Indexing is still not guaranteed. A blocked page, a `noindex` page, or a page whose main text is trapped in an image cannot be the citation, no matter how famous the brand is.

The same document says AI Overviews and AI Mode may use query fan-out: several related searches across subtopics, then a wider set of supporting links than a classic results list. Fan-out is why a narrow page can win a piece of an answer even when you do not rank first for the head term. It is also why a vague page loses. Each sub-question needs a sentence that stands alone. "We are a leading provider of solutions" cannot be that sentence.

Patterns I refuse to ship, because they fail that table:

1. The About page says the company is family-owned and never names a person.
2. Every article ends with the same three tips and no date, so nothing is checkable.
3. The founder is well known on a podcast and the site bio is two sentences that contradict the podcast intro.
4. The homepage claims a result with no source, no year, and no definition of what was counted.
5. The page is one long metaphor. An extractor needs a sentence it can lift without the paragraph before it.

Here is the opinion I will not soften. A plain page with a name, a year, and one checkable receipt beats a press wall of logos that never attach to a sentence. I have watched brands spend the quarter on "thought leadership" while the service page still says "our team of experts." The engine cannot cite a team that does not exist on the page.

Google's February 8, 2023 note from Danny Sullivan and Chris Nelson still holds for me: ranking systems aim to reward original, high-quality content that shows E-E-A-T, and the useful test is Who, How, and Why. Who made this. How it was made. Why it exists. If the why is "to occupy a keyword," the page reads that way, and I do not expect a citation.

Fan-out also punishes sameness. If ten sites repeat one unsourced statistic, the model can pick the oldest copy, the official source, or nobody. I would rather link the official page and add the part only I can add: how I apply it on a site I am willing to put my name on.

## How do I demonstrate Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness to AI systems?

**Put the proof on the page, put a real name on the proof, and get one outside page to repeat the same facts.** That is the order. Tactics that skip the page and start with outreach produce mentions the model cannot attach to a quotable URL on your domain.

Who, How, and Why, from Google's February 8, 2023 post, is the checklist I actually use:

- **Who.** A byline where readers expect one. The same name on the article and on an author or About page. For me that name is William Spurlock, and the company is Spurlock Studios LLC, not a nickname I only use on social.
- **How.** Say what was done in enough detail that a peer could argue with it. Original photos, your own process, and sources for any number that is not yours.
- **Why.** The page should help a buyer decide or do something. If the only reason is to rank, the sentences get empty and the citation goes elsewhere.

The sequence I run on a site that wants to be cited:

1. **Name the legal entity and the person** in the first screen of the About page. William Spurlock. Spurlock Studios LLC. The role: AI Systems Architect and Fractional AI CTO. "AI Visibility Engineer" stays a LinkedIn line. I do not invent a second company name.
2. **Move one receipt onto the page that makes the claim.** 600+ automations built, 500+ live. 20,000+ hours on agentic systems. 35,000+ hours saved for clients. Each number needs the same wording everywhere, or the model will average two biographies and get both wrong.
3. **Write the answer in the first two sentences of every section.** Extractors lift the top of the block. A story that withholds the point until paragraph four is invisible to the citation.
4. **Add the outside confirmation after the page can stand alone.** That is the [digital PR](/blog/digital-pr-for-ai-visibility-getting-mentioned-where-ai-reads) work: a mention that repeats a fact you already published, not a logo with no sentence.
5. **Make the person machine-readable** once the prose is true. Same name, same role, `sameAs` links that resolve. I cover the markup in the [author authority piece](/blog/how-to-build-the-author-authority-ai-looks-for). Markup that describes a person the page never names is a trap.
6. **Keep the brand one entity.** If the homepage, the footer, and the schema disagree on the company name, you have two brands and zero authority. That is the [entity SEO](/blog/entity-seo-how-to-make-your-brand-something-ai-actually-knows) job.

Experience is the letter people fake. "10 years in the industry" is a duration, not an experience. Google's 2022 example wants the person who used the product or visited the place. On my pages that means a specific build, a specific constraint, and what I would not do again. I do not borrow a client name to make the paragraph feel reported.

Expertise is narrower than a content calendar. On YMYL topics I want the credential visible because the rater guidelines and the helpful-content page both raise the bar there. On a marketing-ops topic I want the method. A certificate PNG in the footer does not replace either one.

Authoritativeness is "go-to source," and the September 11, 2025 guidelines are honest that most topics do not have a single official site. When they do, that site wins. Their passport example is the pattern: the government page is the authoritative source for passport renewal. You will not out-authority the issuer. You can be the source for how a buyer in your city should prepare, if you have actually done that work and someone else has said so.

Trust is the kill switch. A page can show experience and still be unsafe to quote if the numbers are made up, the author is anonymous, or a second source calls the company something else. I would rather delete a stat than cite a round number I cannot point at.

## Does having a strong personal brand help AI visibility?

**A personal brand helps when the same name, role, and facts show up on your site and on a profile the model already reads.** A follower count does not. The brand is a consistency check, not a popularity contest.

What I want on the person, in this order:

- The name people search, spelled one way.
- The role you will still use in six months. Mine is AI Systems Architect and Fractional AI CTO.
- The organization, legal name included. Spurlock Studios LLC in public is Spurlock Studios.
- Two or three facts you are willing to repeat forever: the 600+ / 500+ automation split, the 20,000+ hours, the 35,000+ hours saved for clients. Not a new superlative every quarter.
- A page on your domain that states those facts in text, plus one profile you do not host.

A strong personal brand does not outrank a bad page. If my name is known and this article is vague, the citation goes to the specific page, not to me. Fame is a tie-break after the sentence is quotable. I think operators get this backwards because the podcast feels like progress and the About page feels like homework.

There is a real limit. Some queries want the institution, not the founder. The passport office beats the travel blogger. A software vendor's docs beat a commentator when the question is "what does this button do." Personal brand is the right tool when the buyer is choosing a person: a fractional CTO, a studio, a local operator. It is the wrong tool when the buyer needs the issuer.

If you are the person, do the author record properly. I will not re-teach LinkedIn and `Person` markup here. Use [the author authority guide](/blog/how-to-build-the-author-authority-ai-looks-for) for that, then come back and make sure the site still says the same thing.

## Why does AI trust some sites over others on your own pages?

**I audit the page in front of me before I talk about links, podcasts, or a redesign.** Five checks. If the page fails two of them, more content will not fix the trust problem. It will multiply it.

The checks:

1. **Who.** Can I name the author or the responsible company without opening a second tab? Google's Who test, from the February 8, 2023 post, starts here.
2. **Receipt.** Is there one first-hand detail with a date, or only adjectives?
3. **Match.** Does the About page use the same name and facts as the article?
4. **Outside.** Can I find one page I do not control that repeats a fact from this site? If not, the [mention strategy](/blog/digital-pr-for-ai-visibility-getting-mentioned-where-ai-reads) is the next project, not a logo refresh.
5. **Extract.** Can I quote the first two sentences of the main section and have them stay true? If they need the hero animation to make sense, AI Overviews cannot use them. Google's December 10, 2025 AI features note wants important content in text.

I also check eligibility, because trust cannot cite a page Google will not snippet. On December 10, 2025 Google said supporting links in AI Overviews and AI Mode have to be indexed and eligible for a snippet, with no extra technical hoop. So the audit includes `robots.txt`, `noindex`, and whether the useful paragraph is real text. Search Console's Web performance report includes these AI features in the same Web bucket. I do not pretend there is a separate "AI rank" column. There is not, in the docs Google has published.

What I do not audit, because nobody official has given me a number:

- A magic domain-age threshold. The rater guidelines send raters to reputation research, not to a Whois birthday.
- A minimum review count. Volume without a named source is folklore.
- A required Wikipedia page. Some brands will never have one, and forcing it is a good way to get the draft rejected. I treat Wikipedia as a separate question, covered in [does Wikipedia or Wikidata presence affect AI citation](/blog/does-wikipedia-or-wikidata-presence-affect-ai-citation).

When the five checks pass, the redesign is allowed to be beautiful. When they fail, a new visual system just paints the gap. I build the AI-visibility site after the corroboration is real, not before.

## FAQ

### How do press mentions affect your brand authority for AI?

**A press mention helps when it repeats a fact your site already states in public text.** The September 11, 2025 rater guidelines tell raters to look for independent news articles and references about the site or the creator. A logo on your homepage is you talking. A sentence on someone else's site is the outside record. If the article calls you by a different company name than your About page, the mention adds noise. I go further on placement and pitching in [digital PR for AI visibility](/blog/digital-pr-for-ai-visibility-getting-mentioned-where-ai-reads).

### Does media coverage help you get cited by AI systems?

**Media coverage helps you get cited when the article is specific enough for a model to quote, and your own page says the same thing.** Coverage that only says you are "excited to announce" a rebrand gives the engine nothing to lift. Coverage that names the person, the company, and one checkable detail can become the second record in the corroboration test. It does not replace an indexable page on your domain. Google's December 10, 2025 AI features doc still requires your URL to be snippet-eligible if you want your URL in the answer.

### How do I build author authority to get more AI citations?

**Build author authority by using one name, one role, and a short list of facts on your site and on a profile you do not control.** Start with the About page and the byline. Then align LinkedIn and any other profile that already ranks for your name. I keep the step-by-step, including `Person` markup and `sameAs`, in [how to build the author authority AI looks for](/blog/how-to-build-the-author-authority-ai-looks-for). Citations follow the pages that a model can tie to that person, not the pages that merely sound confident.

### Does being featured on podcasts help AI systems recognize your expertise?

**A podcast helps when the show notes repeat your name, your role, and a fact that also appears on your site.** The audio alone is a weak record. Many engines will see the episode title, the guest line, and the notes page. If the host introduces you with a title you do not use, fix your site or send a correction before you treat the episode as proof. I would book the third show only after the first two match the About page word for word on the role and the company.

### Is E-E-A-T a direct ranking factor?

**No. Google says E-E-A-T itself is not a specific ranking factor.** The creating-helpful-content page, updated December 10, 2025, says systems use many factors to identify content with good E-E-A-T, and that rater data is not used directly in ranking algorithms. Elizabeth Tucker wrote on December 15, 2022 that the guidelines do not directly influence ranking. You still write for the qualities, because those are the qualities the systems are built to notice. You do not paste an "E-E-A-T score" into a report.

### Does E-E-A-T matter for ChatGPT and Perplexity, or only Google?

**The letters are Google's. The habit of citing a page you can defend shows up in ChatGPT and Perplexity too.** Google published E-E-A-T for Search raters and for how Search systems are evaluated. OpenAI and Perplexity do not publish an E-E-A-T rubric with those four words. They still attach answers to URLs, and a URL with no author and no source is a bad attachment. I use the same page test everywhere: name, receipt, second source.

### Does an About page with real names help AI citation?

**Yes, when the names match the bylines and the facts are specific.** Google's Who guidance, in the February 8, 2023 post and on the helpful-content page, says people understand E-E-A-T faster when it is clear who created the content, including bylines and background on the author or the site. An About page that lists first names only, or "leadership team" with no roles, does not give a model an entity to store. Put the legal entity on that page as well. For this site that is Spurlock Studios LLC.

### What is the difference between brand authority and topical authority?

**Brand authority is whether the web agrees on who you are. Topical authority is whether your site actually covers the questions in that subject.** You can be a known brand with a thin blog, and you can be an unknown specialist with the best page on one narrow question. Answer engines want both when the query is "who should I hire," and they will take the specialist page when the query is "how does this work." I build the brand record so the name is stable, then the cluster so the adjacent questions have their own pages. This article is the brand-authority pillar. It does not try to be the whole AI Overviews manual.

### How does domain age affect how much AI trusts your content?

**Google does not publish a domain-age number that makes AI trust you.** The September 11, 2025 rater guidelines point raters at reputation: what independent sources say about the site and the creator, including the case where they find nothing. A new domain with a named author and a sourced page can be quoted. An old domain full of anonymous posts can be skipped. I do not wait on a birthday in Whois before I publish a page that can stand a citation.

### What should I show on the page if I want AI systems to treat the brand as real?

**Show a stable name, a reachable person or company, a contact path, and sources next to any hard number.** Trust, in the September 11, 2025 guidelines, fails when the page is not something a rater can rely on, even if the writer sounds skilled. Add credentials when the topic is YMYL or the buyer expects a license. Skip the badge row that no one can verify. If you want the brand stored as an entity, keep the string identical in the title, the footer, and the copy, which is the work in [entity SEO](/blog/entity-seo-how-to-make-your-brand-something-ai-actually-knows).
