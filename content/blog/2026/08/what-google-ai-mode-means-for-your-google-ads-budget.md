# What Google AI Mode Means for Your Google Ads Budget

**The biggest Google Ads budget problem under Google AI Mode is not "AI stole my clicks." It is paying for mid-funnel research queries while your site fails to earn citations in the conversational answers that now absorb that research — then blaming CPC for a visibility gap.** Paid search still works. The mix of queries that deserve paid coverage has changed.

I'm William Spurlock — AI Solutions Architect, Fractional AI CTO, and AI Visibility Strategist. I've spent **20,000+ hours** architecting agentic systems and helped clients reclaim **35,000+ hours** of busywork through automation (separate receipts — personal deep-work hours vs aggregate client time saved). On the visibility side, I've watched Google Ads accounts inflate spend on informational terms while organic and AI-answer surfaces quietly took the comparison stage.

This spoke sits under the pillar [Google AI Mode Explained: How to Rank When Search Becomes a Conversation](/blog/google-ai-mode-explained-how-to-rank-when-search-becomes-a-conversation). For page-level fixes that help you get cited, pair it with [How to Optimize Your Website for Google AI Mode](/blog/how-to-optimize-your-website-for-google-ai-mode). If traffic already slipped after Overviews scaled, start with [Did Google AI Overviews Cause Your Traffic Drop? How to Tell](/blog/did-google-ai-overviews-cause-your-traffic-drop-how-to-tell).

As of mid-2026, Google has described AI Mode as Search for longer, conversational, multimodal questions — with AI-generated responses and links — and at I/O 2026 pointed to [Gemini 3.5 Flash as the default model in AI Mode globally](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/). Google has also framed a path from a results page with an AI Overview into a follow-up conversation in AI Mode ([I/O 2026 announcements](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/ai/google-io-2026-all-our-announcements/)). What Google has *not* published is a tidy "AI Mode changes your CPC by X%" table. Treat third-party CPC shock stories as estimates unless they cite Ads Help, earnings language, or account-level data you can verify.

## What's the biggest mistake businesses make when trying to rank in AI Mode?

**The biggest mistake is treating AI Mode like a paid placement or a keyword bid problem — stuffing ads, schema, and "AI Mode SEO" checklists — while shipping pages that cannot answer a multi-step question in extractable, citable form.** Ranking in AI Mode is citation and passage quality on top of the same crawl/trust systems as classic Search. Pouring more Google Ads budget into research terms does not buy you a citation slot.

I've seen this pattern on accounts that "optimized for AI Mode" by raising bids on head terms and adding FAQ schema with thin answers. Schema without substance is decoration. AI Mode synthesizes; it does not reward a Business Profile badge alone or a generic service page that never states who you serve, where, and what proof you have.

### Mistake vs correct move

| Mistake | What it costs | Correct move |
|---|---|---|
| Bidding harder on informational research queries | Higher CPC for clicks that never close | Cut or tightly cap non-brand research; fund brand + high-intent only |
| Treating AI Mode as a separate "submit to AI" channel | Wasted agency retainers on fake programs | Fix crawlable, answer-first pages on the same index Search already uses |
| Thin FAQ schema with marketing fluff | Zero citations; possible trust drag | FAQ answers with bold lead facts, numbers, and verifiable entities |
| Ignoring AI Overviews → AI Mode handoff | You win neither summary nor follow-up | Optimize for Overview citation *and* deeper conversational passages |
| Measuring only paid CTR / CPA | Blind to unpaid answer theft of research | Track branded search, assisted conversions, and citation presence |

### Why this mistake hits the Ads budget first

Google Ads still shows against many queries. AI Mode and AI Overviews change **which queries still need a paid click** to win the customer.

1. **Research happens inside the answer.** Buyers ask longer questions. As of mid-2026, Google's own product language emphasizes conversational, multimodal questions in AI Mode ([Search I/O 2026](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/)). If your site is not cited, your competitor's page (or a directory) becomes the "trusted" path — then they search your competitor's brand and convert on their ads or organic brand terms.
2. **You keep paying for the questions the answer already resolved.** Mid-funnel CPC stays expensive while conversion rate drops. Teams raise budgets to "defend share of voice" instead of removing the waste.
3. **Brand demand gets misread as "ads are working."** Brand campaigns look healthy because AI Mode trained the buyer on someone else's name. You fund the close for a competitor who won the conversation.

### What "ranking" in AI Mode actually means for budget owners

- **Citation, not position 1.** There is no classic #1 blue link to own inside the synthesis. You want to be a named source with a clear entity.
- **Passage fitness.** Pages that open with a direct answer, then tables/lists, get extracted more cleanly than narrative blog posts that bury the take.
- **Same foundation as Search.** No secret AI Mode index. If Search Console shows crawl/index issues, AI Mode will not rescue you.
- **Paid cannot substitute.** Ads can capture intent that still clicks. They cannot force Gemini-powered synthesis to claim you are the best plumber in Austin if your site never proves it.

### Budget reallocation checklist (run this before raising CPC caps)

| Budget line | Keep / cut / shift | Signal to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Non-brand informational | Cut or experiment-only | High spend, low assisted conversion, rising bounce |
| Non-brand commercial investigation | Shift to landing pages with comparison tables | CPA rising while brand search is flat |
| Brand search | Keep (defend) | Competitor conquesting on your name |
| Local service high-intent | Keep if GBP + site entity match | Calls / form rate by geo |
| Remarketing / customer match | Keep if privacy-compliant | Pipeline stages after AI research |
| "AI Mode awareness" vanity campaigns | Cut | No conversion path, vague creative |

**Opinion:** If your team cannot name which unpaid AI answers are stealing your research stage, you are not ready to increase Google Ads budget. Fix the site first, then defend the paid close.

### A 30-day "stop the bleeding" sequence for Ads + AI Mode

Use this when non-brand efficiency is slipping and leadership wants a number, not a philosophy deck.

**Week 1 — Instrument the truth**

1. Export Search Terms for 90 days. Tag each high-spend term as `research`, `commercial`, `brand`, or `local-urgent`.
2. Build a 25-query citation board: the exact questions your buyers ask that AI Mode can answer in one thread.
3. Screenshot whether you appear as a cited source (or not). No tool required for v1 — honesty beats a fake score.

**Week 2 — Cut informational waste**

1. Pause or cap research themes with CPC above your commercial median and conversion rate below half your account average.
2. Keep brand exact and local-urgent intact unless they are clearly broken.
3. Write down the weekly dollar amount you freed. That number becomes the AI-visibility reinvestment pool — not a silent profit take.

**Week 3 — Fix the pages that should have been cited**

1. Pick three money pages tied to the citation board.
2. Rewrite each opener so sentence one answers the query. Add one comparison table and one FAQ block with bold lead facts.
3. Align GBP categories and site service language so the entity story matches.

**Week 4 — Re-measure without vanity**

1. Compare brand vs non-brand CPA to the Week 1 baseline.
2. Recount citations on the same 25 queries.
3. Only then decide whether to restore any paused themes — and only if assist or CRM stage data justifies it.

This sequence is boring on purpose. Boring is what stops you from "fixing" a citation problem with a Smart Bidding increase.

### How AI Mode changes the buyer sentence (and why your keywords lag)

Classic Search rewarded short strings. AI Mode rewards full sentences and follow-ups. Your keyword list often still looks like 2022:

| Buyer now asks (example) | Legacy keyword you still bid | Mismatch |
|---|---|---|
| "What's a fair price for furnace replacement in Denver if my unit is 18 years old?" | furnace replacement | Answer may finish price framing without a click |
| "Which CRM is better for a 12-person agency that already uses Google Workspace?" | best crm | Synthesis cites 2–3 vendors; ads fight for leftover clicks |
| "Can a mobile detailer come tomorrow morning in Midtown and do ceramic?" | mobile detailing near me | Local + scheduling follow-ups matter more than "near me" alone |

If your RSAs still talk in keyword fragments while AI Mode answers in paragraphs, the unpaid surface will feel more helpful than your ad. That is a creative and landing-page problem before it is a bidding problem.

### What I tell founders who want a simple rule

- **Defend brand.** Always, unless you have a rare monopoly with zero conquesting.
- **Buy urgency.** Phone-intent and "hire / book / quote" still justify auctions.
- **Earn research.** Do not rent it forever at rising CPC.
- **Measure the handoff.** AI Overview → AI Mode is a product path Google has publicly described; your content should survive both the summary and the follow-up ([I/O 2026 announcements](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/ai/google-io-2026-all-our-announcements/)).

If you only remember one line from this section: **ranking in AI Mode is not a Google Ads setting.** It is whether your pages deserve to be quoted when Gemini 3.5 Flash (Google's stated default for AI Mode as of the I/O 2026 Search updates) builds an answer.

## How does Google AI Mode handle local service queries?

**Google AI Mode handles local service queries by synthesizing location-aware answers from Search's local and web signals — Business Profiles, reviews, maps/local packs, and crawlable service pages — then increasingly steering toward task-style follow-ups rather than a pure list of ten links.** Exact ranking weights are not public. As of mid-2026, Google's I/O messaging has pushed AI Mode toward more agentic, task-oriented Search (booking-style and "information agent" language in product coverage), but Google has not published a Help Center formula that maps "plumber near me" → specific AI Mode citation rules. Hedge any vendor claiming otherwise.

For a local service business, that means your Google Ads geo-bid strategy and your AI Mode citation strategy are the same fight: **entity clarity in a place**.

### Local query path: classic vs AI Mode (practical view)

| Stage | Classic local Search | AI Mode-shaped journey |
|---|---|---|
| Query | "emergency plumber Austin" | "Who can fix a burst pipe tonight in North Austin and what's typical?" |
| Primary surface | Local pack + ads + organic | Synthesized answer + citations + follow-ups |
| Trust cues | Stars, distance, ad rank | Named businesses, review consensus, site facts, GBP consistency |
| Next step | Call / click / directions | Follow-up: price range, hours, insurance, same-day |
| Ads role | Capture high-intent clicks | Still capture calls — but fewer research clicks if answer is "done" |

### What local operators should assume (and verify in-account)

- **GBP consistency still matters.** Name, address, phone, categories, and services that disagree with your site confuse every Google surface, including conversational ones.
- **Service-area pages need answers, not city-name spam.** Lead with service + city + proof (licenses, response time, coverage map). AI Mode extracts passages; it does not reward 50 thin city clones.
- **Reviews are comparative fuel.** Synthesis often summarizes "customers say…" patterns. Fake review volume is a risk; real thematic reviews (speed, price honesty, emergency response) are assets.
- **Ads still win the phone for urgent intent.** When someone needs a truck tonight, paid call extensions remain rational. When someone asks a planning question ("best HVAC maintenance plan for a 1998 home"), AI Mode may finish the research without a click — your Ads budget should not pretend that query still needs the same CPC.

### Local Ads budget rules under AI Mode

1. **Separate urgent from research.** Urgent ("near me tonight," "emergency") → paid priority. Research ("best," "vs," "how much should") → site/citation priority; paid only if CPA holds.
2. **Match landing pages to the synthesis gap.** If AI Mode cites directories, your landing page must beat them on specifics: hours, service radius, pricing bands, warranties — not stock photos and "trusted local experts."
3. **Watch branded local search.** Rising competitor brand queries in your geo after you cut content investment is a citation loss signal, not a "brand ads are broken" signal.
4. **Do not buy every city modifier.** AI Mode collapses many near-duplicate local questions into one conversational thread. Duplicate city ad groups often bid against the same intent.

### Local entity checklist for citation + ads alignment

| Element | Site | Google Business Profile | Ads |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary category / services | Exact service language in H1 + body | Matching primary/secondary categories | Keyword themes match categories |
| Service area | Explicit cities / radius | Service areas set correctly | Geo targets match claimed area |
| Proof | Licenses, years, insurance, case notes | Photos, Q&A, posts | Ad extensions with real offers |
| Contact path | Click-to-call, forms, schema | Phone verified | Call tracking numbers consistent in reporting |
| Offer clarity | Pricing bands or "starts at" where honest | Products/services listed | RSAs that state the same offer |

If local organic already softened after Overviews, use the diagnostic frame in [Did Google AI Overviews Cause Your Traffic Drop? How to Tell](/blog/did-google-ai-overviews-cause-your-traffic-drop-how-to-tell) before you "fix" the problem with a 40% budget hike.

### Local query fan-out: what AI Mode is likely synthesizing

When a homeowner asks a conversational local question, the system is not "ranking one URL." It is assembling a shortlist from multiple signal families. You cannot see the exact recipe. You *can* make every signal family agree.

| Signal family | What AI Mode may pull | Failure mode that burns Ads |
|---|---|---|
| Maps / local pack style signals | Proximity, open now, categories | Ads send traffic to a site that claims a wider area than GBP |
| Reviews | Themes: speed, price honesty, cleanliness | Ads promise "same day" while reviews say 5-day waits |
| Web pages | Pricing bands, process, warranties | Thin city pages with no proof — never cited, still paid against |
| Structured facts | Hours, phone, services | Inconsistent NAP → trust drag across surfaces |
| Task / agentic follow-ups | Booking-style next steps (emerging per mid-2026 product direction) | Your site has no clear next action; ads get the click, then bounce |

### Three local landing page patterns that still earn clicks *and* citations

**Pattern A — Urgent repair**

- H1 states service + city + urgency window ("Emergency drain clearing in Austin — often same-day").
- First paragraph: response window, service area, phone.
- Table: after-hours fees vs standard rates (honest bands beat "call for pricing" emptiness).
- FAQ: insurance, what to shut off, typical arrival windows.

**Pattern B — Planned project (HVAC, roof, remodel)**

- H1 states project type + city.
- Comparison table: repair vs replace decision factors.
- Proof block: licenses, manufacturer certifications, photo of real jobs (not stock).
- FAQ: permits, timelines, financing if you offer it.

**Pattern C — Recurring service (cleaning, lawn, pest)**

- H1 states cadence + neighborhood/city.
- Pricing bands or package names that match RSAs.
- Review themes called out in prose ("customers mention punctuality and clear before/after photos").
- Clear booking CTA that matches what Ads promised.

### Local Ads creative rules that match AI Mode answers

If AI Mode tells a user "typical response is same-day for North Austin" and your ad says "Cheapest in town," you lose twice: once in trust, once in Quality Score proxies like landing experience.

| AI Mode tends to surface | Your RSA should | Landing page must |
|---|---|---|
| Hours / open now | State hours or "24/7 emergency line" only if true | Show hours + after-hours policy |
| Service radius | Name the neighborhoods you actually serve | Map or list that matches |
| Price expectations | "Free estimate" or "from $X" if real | Same numbers, no bait |
| Proof / reviews | Specific differentiator, not "best rated" fluff | Reviews + licenses visible above the fold |

### When local paid still beats citation work (and when it does not)

**Paid still wins when:**

- The job is time-critical (flood, lockout, no-heat nights).
- The buyer will call the first credible option.
- Your close rate on calls is strong and tracking is clean.

**Citation / site work wins when:**

- The buyer compares three vendors over days.
- Price and process questions dominate Search Terms.
- You keep winning auctions but losing the shortlist in conversational answers.

Most local accounts need both. The budget mistake is funding only the auction while the shortlist is built from everyone else's pages.

## Does Google AI Mode use your search history to personalize results?

**As of mid-2026, Google has confirmed expanding "Personal Intelligence" and connected-app context in AI Mode (product announcements around I/O 2026), but a clean primary-source statement that AI Mode personalizes using classic search history the way old personalized Search did is not something you should treat as settled fact.** Connected Gmail and other Google apps for personalized context have been part of the public story; **search-history-as-input specifically should be framed as unverified unless Google Search / Account Help states it plainly for your market.** Plan budgets for both personalized and non-personalized journeys.

That ambiguity matters for Ads more than people admit. If two buyers in the same zip see different synthesized shortlists, your aggregate CPC and conversion data will look noisier — and "average CPA" will hide winners and losers.

### What is confirmed vs hedged (mid-2026)

| Claim | Status | Budget implication |
|---|---|---|
| AI Mode is conversational Search on Gemini (incl. Gemini 3.5 Flash default per Google I/O 2026 messaging) | High confidence from Google blog posts | Creative and landing pages must answer multi-step questions |
| Path from AI Overview → AI Mode follow-up | High confidence from Google I/O language | Optimize for both surfaces; paid alone does not cover the handoff |
| Personal Intelligence / connected apps personalization expanding | Confirmed in product announcement coverage | Remarketing and customer-match strategies need privacy-aware rebuilds |
| AI Mode uses search history specifically | Not confirmed in the primary sources I trust for this post — treat as unverified | Do not build a "history targeting" fantasy into your media plan |
| AI Mode publishes a CPC impact formula | Not published by Google Ads Help (as of mid-2026 reporting) | Use your own account deltas; ignore influencer scare charts without methodology |

Sources to re-check as Google updates them: [Search I/O 2026](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/) and [Google I/O 2026 announcements](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/ai/google-io-2026-all-our-announcements/).

### How personalization (even partial) changes paid search measurement

- **Same keyword, different prior context.** Two users can enter similar queries after different AI Mode threads. Your Search Terms report will look "messier" without the conversation history Google does not export to Ads.
- **Brand lift can be invisible in last-click.** Someone gets you cited in AI Mode, then converts on brand paid next week. Last-click says "brand ads work"; the unpaid citation did the education.
- **Audience overlap with logged-in Google users.** If Personal Intelligence expands, logged-in users may see more tailored shortlists. Logged-out or privacy-restricted users may see more generic synthesis. Segment reporting by device / network is incomplete — use CRM stage timing as a second lens.
- **Do not chase "personalized AI Mode ads."** There is no separate AI Mode campaign type that buys personalization. You buy auctions on Search inventory that still exists; you earn citations on content Google trusts.

### Practical measurement stack when personalization is opaque

1. **Baseline a 28-day pre/post window** when AI Mode UI or model defaults change in your market (note the date from Google's blog, not a rumor thread).
2. **Split brand vs non-brand CPA** weekly. Non-brand CPA rising while brand volume rises often means research left the click graph.
3. **Track citation presence manually or with a monitored query set** (20–50 money questions). Log whether you appear as a cited source — even a simple spreadsheet beats guessing.
4. **Pipe call and form quality into the same dashboard as Ads.** AI Mode can increase phone-intent concentration even when click volume falls.
5. **Refuse vanity "AI Mode impressions" from third-party tools** unless methodology is clear. Prefer Search Console, Ads, CRM, and your citation log.

### Personalization myths that burn budget

| Myth | Reality check | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| "If I raise remarketing bids, AI Mode will favor me" | Ads remarketing ≠ AI Mode synthesis preference | Fix entity + content; keep remarketing for known visitors |
| "Search history personalization means I should bid on everything they might ask next" | Unverified mechanism + terrible economics | Map the 10 follow-up questions and answer them on-site |
| "Personalization makes A/B tests useless" | Tests get noisier; they do not become useless | Longer windows, brand/non-brand splits, geo holdouts |
| "Logged-in users always see my ads less" | Inventory and auction dynamics vary; not a universal rule | Watch IS lost (budget/rank) and conversion rate by segment |

**Bottom line for budget owners:** Plan as if some users get a tailored shortlist and some do not. Win both with clear entities and answer-first pages. Do not invent a targeting lever Google has not sold you.

### Privacy settings, logged-out users, and why your averages lie

Even without a definitive "search history fuels AI Mode" statement you can take to a lawyer, personalization *pressure* already exists through account-connected features Google has announced under Personal Intelligence. Practically:

- **Logged-in, apps connected:** more chance the answer uses personal context (email, calendar-style signals — exact fields vary by product rollout and user consent).
- **Logged-out / locked-down:** more generic synthesis from public web + local signals.
- **Your Ads dashboard:** one blended CPA that mixes both cohorts.

That blend is why a single "AI Mode killed our ROAS" narrative is usually wrong. Half the account may be fine. The research cohort is not.

### A privacy-aware testing plan (no fantasy targeting)

1. **Holdout geos.** Keep two similar geos on the old budget mix; move two geos to the cut-waste + citation plan. Compare after 4–6 weeks.
2. **Creative consistency tests.** Keep offer claims identical across RSA and site so personalized shortlists cannot accuse you of bait.
3. **Consent-safe remarketing.** Rebuild lists under current consent rules; do not assume AI Mode "remembers" your ad the way your CRM remembers a lead.
4. **Sales-assisted attribution.** Ask new customers "How did you first hear about us?" for 50 consecutive closes. Cheap. Humbling. Often shows AI answers or "Google told me…" before the paid brand click.

### What personalization does *not* change

| Still true | Still false |
|---|---|
| Crawlable pages with clear entities get cited more often | You can buy an AI Mode citation with a bid |
| Brand defense remains rational | Raising all CPC caps fixes visibility |
| Local NAP consistency matters | Search history targeting is a Google Ads lever you control |
| Answer-first FAQ helps extraction | Thin FAQ schema alone is a strategy |

If a vendor sells you "AI Mode personalization optimization for Google Ads," ask for the Google Ads Help document that defines the inventory. If they cannot produce it, you are buying a story.

## What Google AI Mode means for Google Ads budget in practice

**Treat AI Mode as a demand-shaping layer that compresses unpaid research, not as a reason to abandon Google Ads.** The accounts that stay efficient protect brand and high-intent, cut informational waste, and invest in citation-ready pages so paid dollars close deals that AI Mode already educated.

### Brand vs non-brand under AI Mode pressure

| Segment | Typical AI Mode pressure | Budget posture |
|---|---|---|
| Brand exact | Lower — user already chose you | Defend; watch conquesting |
| Brand + category | Medium — comparison still happens | Keep; align RSA copy with cited claims |
| Non-brand high-intent ("hire," "quote," "near me tonight") | Medium — ads still capture action | Maintain if CPA holds |
| Non-brand research ("best," "how," "vs") | High — answers replace clicks | Cut hard unless proven assist |
| Competitor conquesting | High noise | Use only with strong close rate; else stop |

### When to cut waste (decision table)

| Signal (2–4 weeks) | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Non-brand CPC up, conversion rate down | Research intent leaving the click | Pause informational themes |
| Brand volume up, non-brand down | Answer engines educating elsewhere | Audit citations; keep brand defense |
| Calls up, form fills down | More urgent / local completion | Shift bid strategy to call assets |
| Impression share lost to rank on research terms | Auction competition + weaker QS | Do not "win" those auctions on ego |
| Landing page bounce up on formerly good terms | Query → page mismatch after conversational queries | Rewrite pages answer-first |

### CPC shifts: what you can claim honestly

Google has not published a universal "AI Mode CPC multiplier." As of mid-2026, reports from agencies and industry blogs *suggest* some verticals see higher CPCs on remaining commercial clicks while informational CTR falls — treat those as **estimates**, not law. Your account is the only dataset that pays your bills.

Hedge language you can use with a CFO:

- "We are reallocating from research keywords where AI answers likely absorb the click."
- "We are not claiming a Google-published CPC increase of X%; we are acting on our conversion and Search Terms data."
- "We will reinvest a portion of cut paid waste into AI-visibility site work that earns citations."

That last line is the strategic pivot. Paid and AI Visibility are not rival religions. Paid closes. Citations teach. Teaching without closing wastes organic. Closing without teaching wastes media.

### Sample budget reallocation (illustrative, not a universal prescription)

Assume a $20,000/month Search account that grew up in the "buy every keyword" era. Numbers below are a **worked example** for planning conversations — not a claim about your vertical's averages.

| Line item | Old monthly | New monthly | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand exact | $3,000 | $3,500 | Defend conquesting after AI shortlists |
| Non-brand high-intent | $7,000 | $7,000 | Keep if CPA holds |
| Non-brand research / "best of" | $6,000 | $1,500 | AI Mode absorbs many of these clicks |
| Competitor conquesting | $2,000 | $500 | Keep only proven closers |
| Local-urgent call campaigns | $2,000 | $2,500 | Phone intent concentrates |
| AI-visibility site / content work (from freed media) | $0 | $5,000 | Citation pages, FAQ, entity cleanup |
| **Total** | **$20,000** | **$20,000** | Same envelope, different job |

If leadership will not move dollars from media to site work, you will keep renting the close while competitors own the conversation. That is a capital allocation choice, not a Smart Bidding bug.

### Reporting pack your CFO will accept

Ship a one-pager every month with only these rows:

1. Brand CPA and brand conversion volume
2. Non-brand CPA and non-brand conversion volume
3. Informational Search Terms spend (tagged)
4. Citation board score: X/25 money queries where you appear as a source
5. Call quality: booked jobs / qualified leads from paid
6. Dollars reallocated from research → site work (cumulative)

No heatmaps of "AI Mode impressions" from mystery tools. No invented industry CPC multipliers. Just the account and the citation board.

## FAQ

### What industries are most disrupted by Google AI Mode?

**Local services, professional services, publishers, and comparison-heavy ecommerce feel AI Mode first because buyers ask multi-step "who / what / how much" questions that synthesis can finish without a click.** As of mid-2026, Google's product push toward conversational and more agentic Search increases pressure anywhere the SERP used to monetize research clicks. ### Will AI Mode replace Google Ads?

**No — AI Mode changes which queries still need a paid click; it does not retire the ads auction.** Google's business still monetizes commercial intent. Your job is to stop buying research that no longer clicks and to earn citations so the conversations that remain end on your brand.
