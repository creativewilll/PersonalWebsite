## What is E-E-A-T and why does it matter for AI visibility?

**E-E-A-T is Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness — a quality rubric Google publishes for human raters, and a useful name for the trust check ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews already run when they decide whether your URL is safe to cite.** It is not a plugin. It is not a score you paste into the footer. It is a way to talk about whether a machine can defend naming you.

Google added the extra **E** for Experience on
[December 15, 2022](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2022/12/google-raters-guidelines-e-e-a-t)
in the Search Central post *Our latest update to the quality rater guidelines: E-A-T gets an extra E for Experience*. Elizabeth Tucker wrote that raters now ask whether content shows first-hand use of a product, a visit to a place, or a life experience on the topic — and she repeated that those
[rater guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2022/12/google-raters-guidelines-e-e-a-t)
**do not directly influence ranking**. That date still matters in 2026 because the four-letter frame is what most operators still treat as a Google-only SEO slogan. Answer engines inherited the same problem: they have to pick a source they can stand behind.

Google's
[creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
page (fetched August 2026) is explicit: automated systems look for a **mix of factors** that show experience, expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness; **trust is the most important** of the four; and **E-E-A-T itself is not a specific ranking factor**. I treat that page as the ceiling for Google claims. I do not invent an "E-E-A-T score" ChatGPT stores on your domain.

Why the same letters matter outside Google Search:

| Engine | What "trust" looks like in practice | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| **Google AI Overviews** | A URL Google is willing to show next to a synthesized answer, after the same quality systems that prefer helpful, people-first pages | A separate "E-E-A-T API" you can call |
| **ChatGPT** | A name or URL the model can attach to a real publisher, often after search, without looking like it invented a vendor | A paid placement slot |
| **Perplexity** | A source card it can show because the page is extractable and the publisher looks checkable | A guarantee that a self-published claim is enough |

AI visibility is the recommendation surface. Classic SEO is the blue-link surface. Brand authority is the reason two pages with the same outline do not get treated the same. If the engine cannot answer "who stands behind this, have they done it, and does anyone else agree," it cites the site that already looks like a known publisher.

That is the job of this pillar. Author markup belongs on
[the author-authority how-to](/blog/how-to-build-the-author-authority-ai-looks-for).
Mention campaigns belong on
[the digital PR spoke](/blog/digital-pr-for-ai-visibility-getting-mentioned-where-ai-reads).
Here I stay on the site as a trust object.

**What E-E-A-T is not:** a schema.org type you invent, a ranking factor you "turn on," a longer About page, more blog volume, or a substitute for answering the questions buyers actually ask. Bylines plus a stock headshot is theater. A PR blast with on-site facts that do not match is noise.

## How do Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness show up to an answer engine?

**Each letter shows up as a different class of evidence an engine can extract or corroborate — first-hand receipts, specific category knowledge, third-party recognition of the brand, and a site that does not lie to itself.** Models do not "feel" your reputation. They see text, structured facts, and other pages that repeat or contradict you.

Google's helpful-content page (same
[August 2026 fetch](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content))
says trust is the hub and the other three feed it. That mapping still works when I score ChatGPT and Perplexity answers on audits. A page can win on Experience and lose on Trust if the phone number, offer, or author name conflicts. A page can show Expertise and still get skipped if the publisher looks anonymous.

| Letter | Human meaning | What an answer engine can actually see | Cheap fake that fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Experience** | You did the work, used the tool, stood in the room | Dated first-person process, constraints, failure modes, photos or artifacts you can defend, "what I tested / what I did not" | "We've helped hundreds of clients" with no named work type |
| **Expertise** | You know the category well enough to be specific | Scope, exclusions, definitions, comparison criteria, vocabulary buyers use, pages that answer one job | A 2,000-word rewrite of a competitor's outline |
| **Authoritativeness** | Other people treat you as a known option | Mentions, reviews, citations, and consistent brand strings off-site *and* a site that looks like the same entity | A "As seen in" row of logos you cannot click |
| **Trustworthiness** | Buyers and machines can check you | Named publisher, contact path, honest claims, matching facts, policies, working URLs, schema.org types that match the visible page | Anonymous "we" copy plus a 404 contact form |

schema.org does not create trust. It makes published facts easier to attach to one Organization or Person node. I mention it as a vocabulary, not a tutorial — markup lives on the August 5 spoke. If the visible page says one company name and the JSON-LD says another, you taught the machine two brands.

I do not score "vibe" on an audit. I score extractable blocks:

1. **Who published this?** A real name or a real company string, not "Team."
2. **What did they do, and when?** A receipt I could ask about on a call.
3. **What do they refuse to do?** Exclusions are expertise. Vague "full service" is not.
4. **Does another independent URL say the same brand exists?** One corroborating page beats ten self-claims.
5. **Do the facts match across the homepage, a service page, and the footer?** One mismatch is a leak.

| Block on the page | Engine-readable? | Typical miss on small sites |
|---|---|---|
| Named byline + author or About URL | Yes | Staff alias that exists only on the blog |
| "How we work" with steps and constraints | Yes | Mission paragraph with no process |
| Pricing range or package names you actually sell | Yes, when honest | "Contact for pricing" on every commercial page |
| Reviews with a named outcome | Yes, when specific | Five stars and "great job!!" |
| Organization name, city, and primary URL | Yes | DBA on the homepage, legal name in the footer |
| schema.org `Organization` / `WebSite` that matches the HTML | Yes, if consistent | Plugin dump that conflicts with the theme |

Perplexity makes the source test obvious because it shows cards. ChatGPT often hides the graph and still makes the same bet: cite the publisher it can defend. Google AI Overviews sit on Google's quality systems, which the
[helpful content documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
already describes as hunting for E-E-A-T-shaped factors. Three products. One failure mode: unsigned, uncorroborated, internally inconsistent pages.

For the retrieval-versus-training split — how ChatGPT search and Perplexity actually pick a name — use
[how ChatGPT and Perplexity decide which businesses to recommend](/blog/how-chatgpt-and-perplexity-actually-decide-which-businesses-to-recommend).
That spoke is the mechanism. This pillar is the trust reason the mechanism has something to attach to.

## Why does AI trust some sites over others with similar content?

**Because similar copy is not similar evidence — engines prefer the site whose identity, receipts, and corroboration make the same paragraph safer to quote.** Two "what is GEO" posts can share an outline. Only one of them looks like a publisher a model can point at without guessing.

I see this every week on frozen prompt panels. The losing site is not always thinner. It is often the one that reads like a brochure written by nobody, hosted on a domain that never appears as a source anywhere else. The winning site may have fewer posts and a clearer "this is who we are, this is what we have shipped, this is who it is for."

| Same-looking content | Why the engine still picks B | What A should fix first |
|---|---|---|
| Two how-to posts, same H2s | B names a person, a date, and a constraint A hides | Put a real publisher and a first-hand block on the page |
| Two service pages, same offer | B's brand string matches directories and reviews | Kill the DBA / nickname split on-site first |
| Two FAQ pages, same questions | B's answers are specific enough to lift as a sentence | Replace slogans with extractable facts |
| Two "about us" pages | B can be checked in two clicks; A is a stock photo | Write the identity hub; do not pad word count |
| Two comparison pages | B has criteria and a dated verdict | Stop ranking products you did not evaluate |

The five trust gaps I actually find:

1. **Unsigned claims.** "We recommend X" with no author and no method. An engine that cites you takes the blame if X is wrong.
2. **Identity split.** Homepage name ≠ footer name ≠ schema.org `Organization.name`. That is two entities.
3. **Receipt-free expertise.** Category adjectives with no process, no exclusions, no "last updated."
4. **No corroboration.** The open web never says the brand exists. The June PR spoke is how you earn mentions. This pillar only needs you to see the gap.
5. **Self-contradiction.** Hours, cities, prices, or "since" years that change by URL. Trust dies on the mismatch.

YMYL is Google's name for topics that can hurt health, money, safety, or society. The
[helpful content page](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
says systems give **even more weight** to strong E-E-A-T-shaped factors there. If your advice can cost someone money or health, "similar content" is a fantasy. The engine picks the publisher it can defend.

I refuse to call these trust gaps: a smaller domain than the incumbent (age helps crawl history; it is not a gate), missing Wikipedia, a quiet social account, or a missing E-E-A-T badge. There is no badge. I will not invent a "wait 12 months" rule as a law.

If you want to watch whether the engine's preference is moving, freeze prompts and score mention versus citation versus recommendation using
[how to track when AI tools cite or recommend your business](/blog/how-to-track-when-ai-tools-cite-or-recommend-your-business).
Measurement is a different job. A vendor visibility index is not trust.

## Does a strong personal brand help AI visibility?

**Yes — a consistent, checkable person attached to the company makes the site easier to trust — but a famous founder with a ghost site still loses to a quieter operator whose pages can be verified.** Personal brand is corroboration for the publisher. It is not a substitute for the publisher.

This is the line I will not cross into the August 5 how-to. I am not going to walk LinkedIn fields, Person JSON-LD, or a `sameAs` graph. Those belong there. The question this heading owns is simpler: does a known human help the **site** get cited?

In practice, yes, when the person and the site tell the same story. Buyers ask ChatGPT "who should I hire for X." The model wants a name it can attach to a company, a place, and a track record. A personal brand that matches the site gives it that attachment. A personal brand that conflicts with the site gives it a reason to skip both.

| Personal-brand state | What the engine can do | What I tell the owner |
|---|---|---|
| Named founder, matching About page, matching company string | Attach Person + Organization as one story | Keep publishing under that name; do not invent a house pen name |
| Strong personal following, site still "we" with no humans | Often name the person and miss the company URL | Put the person on the money pages |
| Company-only brand, no public human | Still citable if the org is corroborated | Fine for product companies; weaker for services |
| Two founders with two conflicting bios | Split the entity | Pick canonical strings and stop rewriting from memory |
| Ghostwriter farm, rotating aliases | Treat claims as unsigned | Fewer public authors, maintained |

Personal brand is the wrong project this month if you lack an About page a stranger could check in two minutes, if service pages contradict the homepage offer, if buyers ask a **company** question and you only talk about yourself, or if you are about to start a LinkedIn makeover because it feels like work. That how-to already exists. Come back after the site can stand alone.

A personal brand helps the same way a masthead helps a magazine. Models want to know whose reputation is on the line. A famous masthead over an empty article still does not get quoted.

## What should a small site do this month that is not a LinkedIn makeover or a PR campaign?

**Make the site itself checkable: name the publisher, publish one first-hand page, kill on-site contradictions, and run a short prompt panel so you can see who already gets the trust.** That is a month of operator work. It is not a profile sprint and it is not outreach.

I am leaving LinkedIn, Person schema, and `sameAs` to
[the author-authority how-to](/blog/how-to-build-the-author-authority-ai-looks-for).
I am leaving journalist queries, guest posts, and source-tier targeting to
[digital PR for AI visibility](/blog/digital-pr-for-ai-visibility-getting-mentioned-where-ai-reads).
The list below is what I run when the owner has one operator week and a site that still looks anonymous.

The 30-day site-trust plan:

1. **Pick the canonical brand string.** One public name, one primary URL pattern, one city or service-area phrase. Write it on a note. Every page has to match it.
2. **Name the publisher on the pages that make money.** Bylines or a visible "written by / reviewed by" line that points at a real About URL. Not a staff alias you will abandon.
3. **Rewrite the About page as an identity hub, not a mission.** Who you are, what you sell, who it is for, where, and two receipts you can defend. No invented hours or client names.
4. **Ship one first-hand process page.** "How we run an AI visibility audit" beats "what is AI visibility" if you actually run audits. Date the steps. List what you do not do.
5. **Walk the homepage, two service pages, and the footer out loud.** Any name, phone, offer, or "since" year that changes is a ticket. Fix those before you write post eleven.
6. **Add a contact path a human can use.** A form that works. An email on your domain. A policy page if you collect anything. Trustworthiness is boring on purpose.
7. **Add one extractable table to a buyer page.** Scope vs not-in-scope, packages, or "best for / skip if." Models lift tables. They skip slogans.
8. **Align visible facts with whatever schema.org markup you already ship.** If you do not have markup yet, do not start a schema project this month unless a developer is already on the clock. Conflicting JSON-LD is worse than none. The how-to is the August 5 post.
9. **Run ten buyer prompts in ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews.** Save the answers. Score mention / citation / recommendation. That is your baseline, not a mood.
10. **Fix the one page that should have been the source.** If the engine answered your question from a rival, your page was not safer to cite. Tighten the lead, the table, and the receipt. Do not "content harder" across twenty URLs.

| Week | Site work | Explicitly out of scope |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Canonical strings, About hub, bylines on money pages | LinkedIn rewrite, new social accounts |
| 2 | One first-hand process or proof page + one table | HARO, guest posts, podcast booking |
| 3 | Contradiction sweep (name, offer, city, dates, schema.org vs HTML) | Buying mentions, logo walls |
| 4 | Ten-prompt panel, then repair the one losing URL | A new blog calendar |

Day 30 is done when a stranger can name the publisher, one page exists that only you could have written this year, the homepage and a service page agree on the offer, and you have dated screenshots of who ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews name today.

You will not finish brand authority in a month. You will finish the site-level trust layer that profile work and PR can attach to. Doing those campaigns first is how operators spend a quarter on identity theater while the money pages still say "we."

### How do I build author authority to get more AI citations?

**Build a checkable person-and-site pair, then keep publishing under that pair — the full LinkedIn, Person schema, and `sameAs` sequence is the August 5 how-to, not this pillar.** This page's job is why engines care: they need a publisher they can attach claims to. [how to build the author authority AI looks for](/blog/how-to-build-the-author-authority-ai-looks-for)
when you are ready to make that person machine-readable across profiles.

### Does media coverage help you get cited?

**Yes — independent mentions are Authoritativeness evidence an engine can retrieve — and the tactics for earning them live in the June digital PR post.** A quote on a real publication is a witness. A press-wire dump with no editorial filter is a weaker witness. I will not pretend a single mention flips ChatGPT. I will say a site with zero off-site name use is asking the model to take your word for it.

### How do press mentions affect brand authority for AI?

**Press mentions help when they repeat the same brand string, role, and URL the site already uses; they hurt when they teach a nickname, old company, or dead link.** That is entity hygiene, not vanity. For how to earn those mentions on purpose, use
[digital PR for getting mentioned where AI reads](/blog/digital-pr-for-ai-visibility-getting-mentioned-where-ai-reads).
This pillar only needs the rule: corroboration that matches beats coverage that conflicts.

### Do podcasts help?

**Podcasts help when the episode produces an indexed text page — show notes, a transcript, or a guest bio — that names you the same way your site does.** Audio alone is a weak training and retrieval object. A host page that states your name, company, and a specific claim is a third-party document. Booking the show is PR. Making sure the notes match your canonical strings is trust.

### What is E-E-A-T in one sentence?

**E-E-A-T is Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness — a rater rubric and a practical name for whether an answer engine can defend citing you.** Google documented the extra E on
[December 15, 2022](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2022/12/google-raters-guidelines-e-e-a-t)
and still describes the four aspects on its
[helpful content page](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).
Trust is the hub. The other three feed it.

### Is E-E-A-T a Google ranking factor or an AI-trust model?

**It is not a Google ranking factor; it is a quality framework whose factors overlap the trust checks answer engines run when they pick a source.** Google's own
[creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
documentation (fetched August 2026) says **E-E-A-T itself is not a specific ranking factor**, while systems still use a mix of factors that identify those qualities. The
[December 15, 2022](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2022/12/google-raters-guidelines-e-e-a-t)
rater-guidelines post says those guidelines **do not directly influence ranking**. I use the same four letters as an AI-trust model because ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews still have to decide which site is safe to name. That is a recommendation problem, not a "paste E-E-A-T into title tags" problem.

### Why do ChatGPT and Perplexity cite my competitor when our pages look the same?

**They are not scoring your outline — they are scoring which publisher is safer to stand behind.** Similar H2s hide different evidence: a named author, a dated receipt, matching brand facts, and someone else on the web who already treated that brand as real. Run the same prompt in both engines, list the domains they cite, and compare those trust blocks. The outline was never the exam.

### Can a small site beat a bigger brand on E-E-A-T?

**Yes, on a specific buyer question, when the small site is more checkable and more first-hand than the bigger brand's generic page.** Size helps crawl history and mention volume. It does not automatically make a commodity roundup safer to quote than a dated process page from the person who did the work. I do not invent win rates. I do tell small operators to stop competing on adjective count and start competing on receipts.
