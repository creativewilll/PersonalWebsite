# How to Sell AI Visibility Services: Packaging, Pricing, and Positioning

**You sell AI visibility the same way you sell any serious SEO expansion: name the new win condition, prove the gap with a query bank, then productize the fix into audit + build + retainer tiers — not by rebranding last year's link package as "GEO."** Agencies that win this work in mid-2026 talk about citations inside ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and Google AI Mode the way they used to talk about rankings and CTR. Agencies that lose it either overpromise placements or bury the offer under jargon.

I'm William Spurlock — AI Solutions Architect, Fractional AI CTO, and SEO-certified since 2021. I build AI-visibility-ready sites and help agencies and consultants productize GEO / AEO / AIO work for buyers who still think in blue links. This pillar is written for the **agency and consultant buyer register**: you already run SEO retainers, you see clients' informational CTR soften, and you need a clear way to package, price, and position the second optimization layer without inventing fake ROI.

This is the pillar for the **AI Visibility for Agencies & Consultants** cluster. It answers three questions you get every week — how to sell the work, how to explain GEO to an SEO-only client, and how to package and price it — then expands into offer architecture, objection handling, audit deliverables, measurement that survives a procurement call, and FAQ answers you can reuse in proposals. For the keep / drop / add map your account team should internalize first, see [AI Visibility vs Traditional SEO](/blog/ai-visibility-vs-traditional-seo-what-to-keep-drop-and-add-in-2026). For the content-shape changes you will sell as scope, see [GEO vs SEO: what actually changes in how you create content](/blog/geo-vs-seo-what-actually-changes-in-how-you-create-content).

## How do I sell AI visibility services to my clients?

**Sell the gap between "we still rank" and "we never get named," then attach a productized path from audit → extractability fixes → citation retainer — do not open with acronyms.** Your client already paid for SEO. They do not need a TED talk on Generative Engine Optimization. They need you to show that buyers asking ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google AI Mode for a shortlist never hear their brand, even when Search Console looks fine.

### The sales conversation that works

I coach agencies to run a four-beat conversation. Keep it under twenty minutes on the discovery call; put the receipts in a one-pager afterward.

1. **Surface the symptom they already feel.** Impressions up, clicks down on informational queries. Pipeline from blog softer while brand search holds. Competitors with thinner sites getting named in AI answers.

2. **Name the new win condition.** Traditional SEO optimizes for rank position and click share. AI visibility optimizes for extraction and citation inside synthesized answers. Both still need crawlable pages and real expertise. Only one cares about being the source the answer names.

3. **Prove the gap with a mini query bank.** Pick 15–25 buyer questions. Run them in ChatGPT (with web search where available), Perplexity, Google AI Overviews / AI Mode, and note who gets cited. Screenshot the absences. This is the demo — not a deck.

4. **Offer a path, not a panic rebuild.** Audit (baseline + prioritized fixes) → page and schema sprint (extractability on money URLs) → monthly citation ops (query bank, content, measurement). Price each stage. Never sell "guaranteed AI Overview placements."

### What you are actually selling

| Offer layer | Client outcome | Typical deliverables |
|---|---|---|
| **Diagnosis** | Know where they are invisible | Query bank, citation scorecard, technical/entity gaps |
| **Foundation** | Money pages become extractable | Answer-first rewrites, FAQ, schema, entity cleanup |
| **Content ops** | Steady citation attempts on priority questions | Question-cluster briefs, dual-purpose pages, internal links |
| **Monitoring** | Scoreboard for renewals | Biweekly citation checks, SERP/AI surface notes, CRM-assisted pipeline notes |

If your proposal only sells "more content," you are selling 2021 SEO with a new sticker. If your proposal only sells a monitoring dashboard, you are selling anxiety. Sell the operating system: questions → extractable pages → corroboration → measurement.

### Who buys this fastest

- **B2B services and SaaS** where buyers research with AI before booking demos
- **Local and regional professionals** (legal, medical, home services) losing discovery to answer engines on "best / near me / vs" queries
- **Ecommerce and catalog brands** that need product entities and merchant data readable by AI surfaces
- **Existing SEO retainers** that already trust you — expansion is easier than net-new cold outreach

Who buys slowly: founders who only measure organic sessions and refuse a second KPI. Do not force the sale. Educate, leave the query-bank sample, and revisit after the next CTR dip.

### Positioning lines you can steal (and adapt)

Use plain language. Skip hype framing. Specificity closes.

- "We still protect your Google rankings. We also make sure ChatGPT and Perplexity can name you when a buyer asks for a shortlist."
- "SEO gets you into the results. AI visibility gets you into the answer."
- "Your blog still ranks. The Overview answers the question without clicking you. We fix extractability so you become the cited source."
- "This is not a replacement retainer. It is a second optimization layer on the URLs that already make you money."

For a DIY-shaped proof artifact clients can understand before they sign, point them at [the DIY AI visibility audit: 12 checks](/blog/the-diy-ai-visibility-audit-12-checks-to-see-where-your-business-stands) — then sell your agency version with deeper query coverage, schema validation, and a prioritized sprint plan.

### The proposal structure that survives legal and procurement

1. **Problem statement** in the client's words (CTR, pipeline, competitor mentions)
2. **Definition** of AI visibility vs SEO in one short paragraph
3. **Sample query bank results** (anonymize competitor names if needed; keep screenshots)
4. **Scope by phase** with exit criteria for each phase
5. **Pricing** as ranges or fixed phase fees — never "unlimited GEO"
6. **Measurement** — citation rate on the bank, assisted conversions, branded search as a cross-check
7. **Exclusions** — no guaranteed placements; no buying junk links as "citation building"
8. **Dependencies** — access to Search Console, CMS, brand guidelines, subject-matter experts for E-E-A-T

### Selling into an existing SEO retainer

**Upsell with a parallel workstream, not a rename of the same hours.** Clients smell rebranded line items. I recommend a separate SOW or a clearly labeled addendum:

- Keep classic SEO KPIs on the original retainer
- Add AI visibility KPIs on the expansion
- Share a weekly ritual: 30 minutes on rankings/CTR, 30 minutes on citation scoreboard
- Cap net-new page volume until money URLs are extractable — restructuring often beats publishing for the first 60 days

### Cold outreach vs warm expansion

| Channel | Offer | Proof asset |
|---|---|---|
| Warm SEO client | Audit + 30-day extractability sprint | Their own query bank sample |
| Warm but non-SEO client | Education webinar + paid audit | Anonymized vertical sample |
| Cold agency peer / white-label | White-label audit product | Fixed deliverable checklist + turnaround SLA |
| Cold end client | Diagnostic call + paid audit only | Public checklist + one vertical case pattern (no invented ROI) |

My bias: warm expansion prints faster than cold "GEO agency" branding. Productize the audit first. Marketing polish second.

## What is the best way to explain GEO to a client who only knows SEO?

**Explain GEO as SEO's cousin with a different scoreboard: instead of "rank and get the click," the goal is "get extracted and cited inside the answer."** Same foundations — crawlability, trust, helpful content — different page shape and different proof. If you start with the acronym soup (GEO, AEO, AIO), you lose the room. Start with a side-by-side they already understand.

### The dinner-table explanation

Use this script almost verbatim:

> "Google still has blue links. Your SEO program still fights for those. But a growing share of questions never produce a click — the answer appears in an AI Overview, in AI Mode, or inside ChatGPT or Perplexity. When that happens, the winner is not whoever ranked #1. The winner is whoever the answer names as the source. GEO / AEO work makes your pages easy for those systems to extract and trust. We are not throwing away SEO. We are adding a citation layer."

Then show three screenshots: a classic SERP, an AI Overview, and a ChatGPT or Perplexity answer. Point to citations. Ask: "Where is your brand?" Silence sells.

### Translation table for SEO-native clients

| SEO concept they know | AI visibility equivalent | What changes in the work |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword target | Primary question + FAQ cluster | Briefs start with questions buyers ask AI, not only head terms |
| Meta description / title CTR | Lead-answer sentence under each H2 | First 1–2 sentences must be extractable claims |
| Featured snippet chasing | Answer blocks, tables, FAQs | Structure for machines and skimmers |
| Backlink profile | Corroboration + entity consistency | Earn real mentions; stop selling junk links as GEO |
| Rank tracking | Citation rate on a query bank | Screenshots + logged mentions across surfaces |
| Content calendar by topic | Question-cluster publishing | Pillar/spoke mapped to question banks |
| Technical SEO | Technical SEO + schema/entity hygiene | FAQPage, Organization, Product/Service where relevant |
| E-E-A-T | E-E-A-T with attributable specifics | Named experts, dated sources, client-safe receipts |

For the deeper content fork — what writers change day to day — send them [GEO vs SEO: what actually changes in how you create content](/blog/geo-vs-seo-what-actually-changes-in-how-you-create-content) after the call. Do not make them read it before they understand the scoreboard.

### GEO vs AEO vs AIO — only if they ask

Keep this short. Clients care about outcomes, not taxonomy wars.

- **GEO (Generative Engine Optimization):** optimizing to be cited/recommended inside generative answers (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini experiences, etc.).
- **AEO (Answer Engine Optimization):** optimizing for answer-shaped surfaces — featured answers, AI Overviews, voice-style Q&A.
- **AIO (AI Overview / AI Optimization, depending on who is talking):** in practice on this site, the work that makes pages ready for Google's AI features and answer extraction generally.

In proposals I often use **AI visibility** as the umbrella and mention GEO/AEO only in a footnote. Your client's CFO does not need the history of the terms.

### Analogies that land (and one that fails)

**Works:** "SEO is shelf placement in the store. AI visibility is getting named by the clerk when someone asks what to buy."

**Works:** "Rankings are your billboard on the highway. Citations are the recommendation your friend texts when someone asks for a vendor."

**Fails:** "SEO is dead, GEO is the future." You will trigger defensiveness from every SEO lead in the room — and you will be wrong. See the keep/drop/add argument in [AI Visibility vs Traditional SEO](/blog/ai-visibility-vs-traditional-seo-what-to-keep-drop-and-add-in-2026).

### A 10-minute live demo agenda

1. **Minute 0–2:** Ask the client for five questions their buyers actually ask before purchasing.
2. **Minute 2–6:** Run those questions live across two AI surfaces + Google.
3. **Minute 6–8:** Show one competitor page that got cited — highlight answer-first structure, tables, FAQ, clear entity.
4. **Minute 8–10:** Show their closest money URL — highlight buried answers, thin FAQ, missing schema, vague claims.
5. **Close:** "The fix is not more blog posts first. It is extractability on these URLs, then a question bank we operate monthly."

### Objections you will hear in this explanation

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Our rankings are fine." | "Rankings and citations are different KPIs. Let's check the query bank." |
| "AI just steals our content." | "Some clicks compress. Being the named source is how you stay in the shortlist when clicks shrink." |
| "Isn't this just featured snippets again?" | "Related skill, bigger surface area — ChatGPT, Perplexity, AI Mode, not only position zero." |
| "Can we wait a year?" | "You can. Competitors who become the default cited brand will be harder to displace later." |
| "We already use AI to write content." | "Generating drafts is not the same as designing pages for extraction and trust." |

### Teaching their content team without a mutiny

Writers fear "write for robots." Reframe: write the answer a human would screenshot, then support it with proof. The [AI visibility content strategy](/blog/the-ai-visibility-content-strategy-writing-for-humans-and-answer-engines) post is a good handoff document for in-house editors. Pair it with a one-page internal standard:

- H2 = a real question
- First sentence = direct answer in bold
- Include one structured element (table, list, steps)
- FAQ section with real questions
- Claims sourced or hedged
- Internal links only to live URLs

Models in the workflow (Claude Sonnet 5 / Claude Opus 4.8 for harder pages, GPT-5.5 / GPT-5.4 mini for outlines, Gemini 3.1 Pro / Gemini 3.5 Flash for research synthesis, Llama 4 for cost-sensitive loops) accelerate drafts. Humans own opinions, receipts, and client-safe specifics. Say that out loud so clients do not think you are selling unreviewed AI mush.

## How do I package and price AI visibility services?

**Package AI visibility as three productized tiers — Audit, Foundation Sprint, and Citation Retainer — and price them as market-observed ranges tied to scope, not as a mysterious "GEO multiplier" on SEO hours.** Buyers understand phases. They do not understand "strategic AI presence." Put deliverables, cadence, and exit criteria on one page.

### Recommended offer architecture

#### Tier A — AI Visibility Audit (productized, entry)

**Job:** baseline + prioritized backlog your client can buy even if they do not retain you yet.

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Query bank | 25–50 buyer questions, tagged by funnel stage |
| Multi-surface check | Google AI Overviews/AI Mode, ChatGPT, Perplexity (document date + model/UI notes) |
| On-site extractability review | Top 10–20 money URLs |
| Technical / schema / entity pass | Crawl issues, FAQPage/Organization/Product gaps, entity consistency |
| Competitive citation sample | 3–5 competitors on the same bank |
| Deliverable | Scorecard + 30/60/90 backlog + optional SOW for Tier B |
| Timeline | 1–3 weeks depending on site size |

**Pricing I have seen in mid-2026 (observations, not a price list you must copy):** roughly **$2.5k–$8k** for SMB / single-location service businesses; **$8k–$20k+** for multi-location, ecommerce, or complex B2B sites. Complexity drivers: number of URLs, locales, product catalog size, and how many AI surfaces you include. Hedge hard — your market, brand, and delivery costs differ.

#### Tier B — Foundation Sprint (30–60 days)

**Job:** make money URLs extractable before you scale publishing.

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Page rewrites | Answer-first H2s, tables, FAQs on priority URLs |
| Schema implementation | Valid JSON-LD where it matches visible content |
| Entity cleanup | About/Organization consistency, author pages, sameAs hygiene |
| Internal linking | Pillar/spoke paths to commercial URLs |
| Measurement setup | Citation log template + reporting cadence |
| Exit criteria | Priority URL checklist complete; query bank re-tested |

**Pricing I have seen:** often **$6k–$25k** as a fixed sprint, or a **$4k–$12k/month** intensive for 1–2 months. Ecommerce feed/schema-heavy work can run higher. Again: market observation, not a promise.

#### Tier C — Citation Retainer (ongoing)

**Job:** operate the question bank, ship dual-purpose content, re-test citations, protect commercial URLs.

| Monthly block | Example allocation |
|---|---|
| Query bank ops | Biweekly re-checks on priority questions |
| Content | 2–6 question-cluster pages or major refreshes (scope-bound) |
| Technical hygiene | Schema QA, crawl fixes, template improvements |
| Reporting | Citation rate, notes on surface changes, next sprint picks |
| SEO coordination | Keep classic SEO work from colliding with AI visibility edits |

**Pricing I have seen:** retainers commonly land around **$3k–$10k/month** for focused SMB programs; **$10k–$25k+/month** when you are running multi-market content ops plus engineering support. White-label delivery to other agencies often prices lower per end client but needs stricter SOPs. Treat these as ranges I have observed across conversations and proposals in the market — not guaranteed benchmarks and not named-client ROI.

### Packaging rules that prevent scope chaos

1. **Cap questions in the bank.** 25–50 active questions beats 500 ignored ones.
2. **Cap URLs in the sprint.** Top commercial + top informational money pages first.
3. **Separate SEO hours from AI visibility hours** in the invoice narrative.
4. **Write exclusions.** No guaranteed Overview placement. No link buying. No "we control ChatGPT."
5. **Define revision rounds** on rewrites.
6. **Require SME access** for E-E-A-T — or the content will be generic and fail citation tests.

### Good / Better / Best menu (one-pager friendly)

| | Good | Better | Best |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry | Audit only | Audit + Foundation Sprint | Audit + Sprint + 6-month retainer |
| Surfaces tracked | Google + one AI chat | Google + ChatGPT + Perplexity | Multi-surface + competitor panel |
| Content | Recommendations only | Rewrites on top URLs | Rewrites + monthly cluster publishing |
| Engineering | Issues list | Schema implemented | Schema + template-level extractability |
| Reporting | Scorecard | Scorecard + monthly | Biweekly ops + quarterly strategy |

Clients like menus. Engineers like caps. Give both.

### Pricing psychology for SEO agencies

- **Anchor on risk, not on hours.** Hours invite comparison to cheap freelancers. Risk framing: "informational discovery is moving into answers; we measure and repair citation gaps."
- **Phase fees beat blended mystery retainers** for net-new buyers.
- **Retainers beat phases** once the scoreboard exists and the client sees movement — or at least a clear operating rhythm.
- **Do not discount monitoring-only.** Monitoring without fixes trains clients to hate the dashboard.
- **White-label:** sell deliverable clarity; protect your brand with anonymized methods if needed.

### Sample pricing narrative (safe language)

> "For a single-location service business with a focused site, I've seen AI visibility audits quoted in the low-to-mid thousands, foundation sprints in the high four to low five figures, and retainers that sit beside SEO rather than replacing it. Multi-location and ecommerce programs run higher because entity and catalog complexity rises. We will quote fixed phase fees after we see your URL list and query bank size — not before."

That paragraph is honest. It does not invent a case study ROI. Use it.

### Bundling with SEO without confusing the P&L

| Approach | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Separate SKUs | Clean KPIs, easier upsell math | Two invoices / two conversations |
| Combined "Search + AI Visibility" retainer | Simple for client | Harder to defend scope; easy to under-deliver |
| Audit as paid diagnostic before retainer | Filters tire-kickers | Longer sales cycle |
| Performance bonus on citation rate | Aligns incentives | Attribution fights; avoid unless measurement is mature |

My default recommendation for most agencies: **paid audit → sprint → retainer**, with SEO kept as a sibling SKU.

### What to put in the price calculator (internal)

- Number of priority URLs
- Number of locales / languages
- Catalog size (SKU count) if ecommerce
- Who implements schema (you vs client eng)
- Content production (you write vs you brief their writers)
- Meeting load (weekly vs biweekly)
- Legal/compliance review cycles (YMYL verticals)

If you ignore YMYL review time, you will lose money on healthcare and finance retainers.

## Objection handling: the scripts that keep deals alive

**Most AI visibility deals die on four objections — guaranteed results, "SEO is fine," budget collision, and fear of AI writing slop — and each has a calm, specific answer.** Memorize these. Do not argue philosophy.

### "Can you guarantee we show up in ChatGPT / AI Overviews?"

**No. Decline guarantees.** Say:

> "No ethical operator can guarantee a specific AI surface will cite you on a timeline. Models, UI, and Overview triggers change. What we guarantee is the operating system: a query bank, extractable pages, valid structured data where appropriate, corroboration work, and a measurement cadence. That is how citation rate moves over time."

If a prospect demands guarantees, they are a bad-fit client. Let a competitor take the lawsuit risk.

### "Our SEO is working — why add this?"

> "If commercial pages and brand search are healthy, great — keep funding that. AI visibility is about the questions that used to send informational clicks and now resolve inside answers. We will show you the query bank sample. If you are already cited everywhere that matters, you do not need us yet."

Then show the sample. Either they have a gap or they do not.

### "We do not have budget beyond SEO."

> "Then start with a paid audit and a narrow foundation sprint on the top five money URLs. Do not buy a bloated retainer. Reallocate from low-yield informational volume if needed — fewer, better pages often beats more posts that never get cited."

Help them cut waste. Do not shame them for protecting SEO spend.

### "Isn't this just AI content spam?"

> "If someone ships unreviewed model drafts with no receipts, yes — that fails. Our standard is answer-first structure, human expertise, sourced or hedged claims, and pages a subject-matter expert would defend on a sales call. Tools like Claude Opus 4.8 or Claude Sonnet 5 can accelerate drafts. They do not replace your experts."

### "Our competitor hired an 'AI SEO' tool — should we?"

> "Buy software after the ritual exists. Dashboards that narrate your absence without a fix backlog are expensive anxiety. We install the query bank and extractability work first; tools second."

### "Will this hurt our rankings?"

> "Done correctly, answer-first structure, clearer entities, and better FAQs usually help traditional SEO. It hurts when teams ship thin near-duplicates or break templates with sloppy schema. We coordinate with your SEO workstream and protect commercial URLs."

Point them to the keep/drop/add logic in [AI Visibility vs Traditional SEO](/blog/ai-visibility-vs-traditional-seo-what-to-keep-drop-and-add-in-2026) if they want depth.

## The sales process: from first call to signed retainer

**Run a diagnostic-led process: educate → paid audit → sprint → retainer. Skip the giant strategy theater.** Agencies that sell six-week "discovery" before any artifact create distrust. Ship a scorecard early.

### Stage map

| Stage | Goal | Artifact | Exit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Discovery (30–45 min) | Confirm symptom + fit | Call notes + sample questions | Paid audit proposal |
| 2. Paid audit | Baseline truth | Scorecard + backlog | Sprint SOW or polite no |
| 3. Foundation sprint | Extractability on money URLs | Before/after pages + re-test | Retainer decision |
| 4. Retainer | Ongoing citation ops | Monthly scoreboard | Renewal on operating value |

### Discovery call checklist

- What does pipeline from organic look like vs 12 months ago?
- Which queries used to convert that feel softer now?
- Who is the buyer, and do they research with AI tools?
- What CMS and engineering access exist?
- Is there an in-house SEO lead who will feel threatened? (Plan the joint narrative.)
- Any YMYL constraints?
- Success definition in their words (demos, calls, stores visits, online revenue)

### Red flags to walk away from

- Demands guaranteed AI Overview placement
- Wants you to block all AI crawlers while also "winning GEO"
- Refuses any measurement beyond vanity screenshots
- Expects you to fabricate case-study ROI for their deck
- Will not give SME access for expert content
- Wants link schemes rebranded as citation building

Walking away is packaging. Bad clients destroy delivery margins and case-study quality.

## What an agency-grade AI visibility audit should include

**Your audit is the productized wedge — make it boringly clear, visually scannable, and impossible to confuse with a generic SEO audit PDF.** Clients should finish reading knowing exactly what is broken, what to fix first, and what it costs to continue with you.

### Minimum viable audit sections

1. **Executive summary** — three findings, three priorities, one recommended path
2. **Query bank results** — table with question, surface, cited brands, your status (cited / mentioned / absent)
3. **URL extractability review** — money pages scored on answer-first, structure, FAQ, schema, entity clarity
4. **Technical & crawler notes** — indexation, `robots` / AI crawler posture, performance on key templates
5. **Entity & corroboration** — knowledge consistency, about page, authors, third-party mentions
6. **Competitive panel** — why someone else gets named
7. **30/60/90 backlog** — effort vs impact
8. **Measurement plan** — how you will prove progress without fake ROI
9. **Commercial next step** — sprint scope and price range

For a client-friendly checklist you can share pre-sale, align your scoring with [the DIY AI visibility audit](/blog/the-diy-ai-visibility-audit-12-checks-to-see-where-your-business-stands) and deepen it with proprietary query coverage.

### Scoring rubric (example)

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead answer present | Buried or missing | Present but weak | Clear, bold, specific |
| Structured elements | None | Lists only | Tables/steps/comparisons |
| FAQ quality | None / fluff | Some real Qs | 6–10 real Qs answered tightly |
| Schema match | None / invalid | Partial | Valid and matches visible content |
| Entity clarity | Confusing | Partial | Consistent across key URLs |
| Corroboration | Invisible off-site | Thin | Credible mentions exist |

Sum scores per URL. Priority = low score × high commercial value.

### How to present without overwhelming

- Lead with the scorecard, not the crawl dump
- Show five example questions live if you can
- Put the full URL appendix behind the summary
- Always end on a decision: sprint yes/no

## Measurement and ROI conversations without inventing numbers

**Demonstrate progress with citation rate, assisted conversions, and branded search cross-checks — never with invented "300% AI ROI" slides.** Procurement teams are getting smarter. Fake precision will burn your agency.

### Primary metrics

| Metric | How to capture | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Citation rate | % of query bank where brand is cited/mentioned | Core AI visibility KPI |
| Share of answer | How often you appear vs named competitors | Competitive framing |
| Extractability score | Rubric on priority URLs | Leading indicator |
| Assisted conversions | CRM notes / UTMs / "how did you hear" | Business bridge |
| Branded search | Search Console brand queries | Proxy for recommendation spillover |
| Classic SEO health | Rankings/CTR on commercial URLs | Prove you did not torch SEO |

### Secondary metrics (use carefully)

- Referral traffic from AI products when identifiable
- Engagement on answer-first pages (scroll, CTA)
- Newsletter/demo conversions from citation-driven journeys (hard to attribute — say so)

### ROI narrative that stays honest

> "We do not claim a universal ROI multiple for AI visibility work. ## Internal FAQ for account executives

**Train AEs on these so they stop improvising dangerous promises.**

### What if the client asks for a free audit?

**Offer a free 10-question sample, not a full audit.** Full audits are the product. Free full audits train the market to undervalue diagnosis.

### What if they want us to block AI crawlers?

**Discuss tradeoffs explicitly.** Blocking can limit training/use of content in some systems while also reducing the chance those systems cite you. Align on goals before touching `robots` rules. Do not make unilateral changes.

### What if their SEO vendor feels threatened?

**Invite them into the kickoff. Position as a complementary layer.** Share the keep/drop/add framing. If the other vendor badmouths citation work as fake, stick to the query bank evidence.

### What if leadership only cares about sessions?

**Reframe to pipeline and shortlist inclusion.** Offer a dual dashboard: classic SEO metrics + citation metrics. If they refuse a second KPI after education, they may not be ready.

### What if a junior marketer wants 50 AI posts per month?

**Cap volume. Sell extractability on money URLs first.** Volume without structure creates sludge and can dilute entity clarity.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does an AI visibility audit look like for a client deliverable?

**An agency-grade AI visibility audit is a scorecard plus backlog: a 25–50 question citation bank across Google AI surfaces, ChatGPT, and Perplexity; extractability scores on money URLs; schema/entity findings; a competitive panel; and a 30/60/90 plan.** It should read in fifteen minutes at the executive layer, with appendices for specialists. If the PDF is only a crawl export with "GEO" on the cover, it is not an AI visibility audit.

### How do I demonstrate ROI of AI visibility work to clients?

**Demonstrate ROI with citation-rate movement on agreed buyer questions, extractability improvements on commercial URLs, and assisted pipeline your client can verify — not with invented percentage returns.** Tie reporting to their CRM reality. Estimates vary by vertical and cycle length; as of mid-2026, treat branded search and sales anecdotes as cross-checks, not as sole proof. Refuse clients who demand a guaranteed revenue multiple up front.

### Should I rebrand my SEO agency to include GEO and AEO services?

**Usually add a service line first; only rebrand if your current name actively blocks sales.** Most firms close faster with "SEO + AI Visibility" positioning, proof from audits, and clear packages than with a full rename. Rebrand when category perception is stuck and you already have delivery proof — not as a substitute for a productized offer.

### What is a fair price to charge for AI visibility optimization in 2026?

**From market conversations and proposals I have seen in mid-2026, paid audits often land roughly in the low thousands to low tens of thousands, foundation sprints in the high four to mid five figures, and monthly retainers commonly somewhere in the low-to-mid thousands up through higher five figures for complex programs — with wide variance by scope.** These are observations, not universal rates. Price from URL count, locales, catalog complexity, engineering ownership, and YMYL review load. Hedge every public number; quote fixed fees after discovery.

### How is selling GEO different from selling traditional SEO retainers?

**You sell a second scoreboard — citations inside answers — and a different content shape, while still protecting classic rankings.** The discovery asset is a query bank sample, not only a rank report. Packages skew toward audit → extractability sprint → citation ops, because publishing volume alone underperforms when pages are not extractable. Measurement must include citation logs alongside Search Console.

### What should I include in an AI visibility monthly retainer?

**Include query-bank operations, a capped number of answer-first pages or refreshes, schema/technical hygiene, citation reporting, and coordination with SEO so commercial URLs stay protected.** Exclude guarantees and unlimited production. If the monthly pack is only screenshots, you are selling anxiety. Ship work and a scoreboard.

### How do I handle clients who want guaranteed ChatGPT mentions?

**Say no, explain volatility, and reframe guarantees around operating system delivery: bank, extractability, schema hygiene, cadence, and measurement.** If they insist, walk away. Guaranteed mention clauses create legal and reputational risk you cannot control when model UIs and Overview triggers change.

### Can I white-label AI visibility services for other agencies?

**Yes — productize audits and sprints with SLAs, partner-facing templates, and strict rules against overpromising.** White-label works when the partner owns the relationship and you own delivery quality. Train their AEs on the GEO explainer and objection scripts so they do not sell miracles your team cannot ship.
