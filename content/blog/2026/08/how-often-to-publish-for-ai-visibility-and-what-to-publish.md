## How often should a small business publish for AI visibility?

**Most small businesses should publish one or two citation-worthy units per week, not a daily blog and not a 30-URL dump, and they should count a money-page rewrite or a real refresh as a publish.** ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and Google AI Mode do not award you for showing up on Friday. They retrieve pages that already look like answers. A thin URL you cannot defend is not a unit. It is inventory you will have to clean later.

I use "unit" on purpose. A unit is one of three things you can stand behind:

1. **A new spoke** that owns a buyer question no other URL on your site already owns.
2. **A refresh** of a page that already has impressions, links, or a query you still want.
3. **A money-page rewrite** — service, offer, "who we are for," pricing, or location — when that page cannot survive a hire/buy prompt.

A 400-word "thoughts this week" post is not a unit. If you cannot name the query the URL is supposed to win, it does not go on the calendar.

| Site shape | Default cadence | What a week looks like | What I refuse |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solo operator, under 20 real pages | 1 unit / week | Alternate new spoke and refresh; money page first if it is broken | Daily posting to "stay consistent" |
| Small team, 20–80 pages, one cluster | 1–2 units / week | One new spoke + one refresh or money pass | Two new posts that share a primary query |
| Agency / newsroom with editors | 3–5 units / week only if QA holds | Brief → edit → ship; refresh queue stays full | 30 thin AI drafts with no owner |
| Local service with 5–8 money URLs | 2–4 units / month | Fix the five money pages, then one spoke | A blog that ignores the service pages |

That table is an operating default, not a ranking factor. Google has not published "post twice a week for AI Overviews."

### Why I will not tell you to publish every day

Daily publishing helps a site that already has editors and a freeze on duplicate primaries. It hurts a five-page operator because the next URL is almost always a restatement of a page you already have. Google's
[spam policy on scaled content abuse](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#scaled-content)
calls out generating many pages whose primary purpose is manipulating rankings, not helping users — "no matter how it's created" — including "using generative AI tools… to generate many pages without adding value." A daily habit of interchangeable posts is that pattern at small-business scale.

The same ceiling is in Google's
[guide to optimizing for generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide):
pages spun for every query variation to manipulate rankings or generative responses fall under that policy, and **"a high quantity of pages doesn't make a website higher quality."**

### The only frequency that is actually load-bearing

**Re-run a frozen prompt panel on a calendar.** Weekly if you are in a fight; monthly if you are not. That is observation cadence, not publishing cadence. The
[tracking post](/blog/how-to-track-when-ai-tools-cite-or-recommend-your-business)
owns the scoring. Shipping five posts and never re-asking ChatGPT the same 20 buyer questions is theater.

Ahrefs' [July 28, 2025 freshness study](https://ahrefs.com/blog/do-ai-assistants-prefer-to-cite-fresh-content/) of about **17 million** cited URLs found AI assistants cited pages that were, on average, **25.7% newer** than organic Google results (1,064 days vs 1,432 days since first seen). ChatGPT leaned newest; Google AI Overviews sat closer to organic age. The average cited URL was still about **2.9 years** old. Ahrefs also repeated John Mueller's caution: do not bump dates without a real change. That is why a refresh slot exists. It is not a reason to stamp "updated" on 40 posts every Monday.

## What should you publish first: a new post, a refresh, or a money-page rewrite?

**Rewrite the money page first if a hire/buy prompt would not name you from that URL; refresh next if an existing page already owns the query and has gone stale; write a new post only when the buyer question has no owner on the site.** Most small sites invert this. They blog because blogging feels like marketing.

I pick the week's unit with three questions, in this order:

1. **Can a buyer who already wants the job decide from our money pages?** If the service page cannot say who it is for, who should skip it, what the work costs in a range you can defend, and what happens in week one, that rewrite is the publish.
2. **Do we already have a URL for this question?** If yes, do not open a new file. Put a refresh on the calendar. How to do the rewrite lives in
   [the refresh framework](/blog/refreshing-old-content-for-the-ai-era-a-practical-framework).
   The decision that the slot is a refresh lives here.
3. **Is this a new buyer question we can answer with receipts?** If yes, that is a spoke. Shape it with the
   [question-first model](/blog/the-question-first-content-model-that-gets-you-cited-by-ai).
   Do not start a second spoke on the same primary query.

| This week's evidence | Ship this | Do not ship |
|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT names a rival on "who should I hire for [job]" and your service page is a hero paragraph | Money-page rewrite | A new "what is [category]" blog |
| Search Console shows impressions on an old URL; the facts or the lead answer aged out | Refresh that URL | A duplicate post with a new slug |
| Sales keeps hearing a question no URL owns | New spoke | Another pass on the homepage |
| You have three posts aimed at the same question | Merge / refresh the strongest; stop publishing variants | A fourth URL "from a new angle" |
| The only idea on the board is a weekly "best X" list | Skip the week, or write one comparison you will maintain | A listicle factory — craft is a different post |

### Money pages count as publishing

Operators tell me "we publish weekly" and then show me a blog while `/services` still reads like a brochure. Hire prompts resolve against the page that states the offer. If that page is vague, a brilliant spoke gets you a footnote and the rival still gets the recommendation.

A money-page week I will accept as a full unit:

- **Who we serve / who we do not** in sentences a model can lift.
- **Scope and next step** a buyer can act on (intake, audit, package) — not "contact us for details" as the only fact.
- **Proof that is dated or hedged.** No invented conversion multiples.
- **Internal links** to the one or two spokes that already support the offer.

That is publishing. It is also usually higher ROI than post number 14 about industry trends.

### New vs refresh is an equity test, not a mood

Write new when the question is unowned. Refresh when the URL has equity and the intent still matches. Equity is impressions, backlinks, a query you still want, or a page that already appears in a Perplexity drawer. If none of those are true and the topic still matters, a new canonical with a redirect can be the honest move — once, not as a weekly habit.

## Does a weekly blog help ChatGPT and Perplexity cite you, or does it just add thin pages?

**A weekly blog helps when each post is a distinct, extractable answer to a buyer question you did not already own. A weekly blog is just thin pages when the cadence is a habit and the URLs are interchangeable.** ChatGPT and Perplexity do not have a "consistency" bonus for publishers. They have retrieval. Retrieval likes a page it can quote. It does not like a folder of near-duplicates.

I have watched small sites add 40 posts in a quarter and lose the plot: the homepage still cannot say who the firm is for, the money queries still cite a directory, and the new URLs compete with each other. That is a CMS habit.

| Weekly pattern | What the engines see | Typical outcome |
|---|---|---|
| One dense spoke, one primary query, tables and a real FAQ | A page that already looks like an answer | Possible citation if third-party proof exists |
| One money-page rewrite, blog quiet that week | A clearer entity + offer | Often the first recommendation movement I see |
| One honest refresh of a page that already ranks or gets impressions | A newer extractable answer on an old URL | Freshness helps as a tie-breaker, not a cheat code |
| Four 700-word posts that restate the same category | A cluster of commodity pages | Thin inventory; scaled-abuse risk if you keep going |
| A "best X" every Friday | A storefront or a directory, unless it is a scored page you maintain | Usually a skip; do not use this spoke to learn comparison craft |

### What "thin" means on a calendar

Thin is not "short." Thin is **no new decision for a buyer and no new fact an engine can attribute.** You need a reason the URL exists that is not "we had a publishing slot."

Signs the weekly blog is adding thin pages:

- You could swap the H1 with a competitor's H1 and the body would still read true.
- Two posts on your site would both claim the same primary query in a prompt panel.
- You published because the calendar was empty, not because sales, support, or the panel surfaced a gap.
- You could not describe the next refresh you owe that URL.
- The only metric you report is "posts shipped."

Ahrefs' [March 2, 2026 AI Overview citation study](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10/) (863,000 keyword SERPs, about 4 million AI Overview URLs) found only **37.9%** of cited URLs also appeared in the first 10 organic blocks for the same query, down from about **76%** in their July 2025 cut. Ranking more pages for head terms is a weak plan for Google AI Overviews. Adding weekly posts that chase the same head term is how you grow an index nobody quotes.

### Weekly can still be the right cadence

I run a weekly unit on this site. The difference is ownership: one primary query per URL, a refresh queue, and a stop when the next idea is a duplicate. Weekly is a **capacity setting**, not a citation tactic. If the week only produces a thin page, skip it. A skipped week is cheaper than a URL you will merge in six months. Do not confuse a publishing streak with a ChatGPT or Perplexity recommendation.

## What mix of questions should a month of publishing cover?

**A month of publishing should cover a hire/money question, one or two unowned category questions, at most one local or constraint question if that is how you sell, and one or two refreshes of questions you already claim — not four posts in the same funnel slot.** How to write the H2 is the parent
[question-first model](/blog/the-question-first-content-model-that-gets-you-cited-by-ai).
This section is which **slots** get a unit.

| Slot | Job the unit does | Share I use on a 4-unit month | Skip the slot when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Money / hire | Make the offer extractable: who it is for, what you will not do | 1 | The money pages already survive a hire prompt |
| Unowned category question | New spoke for a question with no URL | 1–2 | You cannot answer it with receipts |
| Local or constraint | Place, budget, timeline, or "skip if" the buyer actually uses | 0–1 | You are not local and have no constraint that changes the pick |
| Proof / "is it worth it" | Risk, fit, or "should a [buyer type] bother" | 0–1 | You would be inventing outcomes |
| Refresh of an owned question | Keep a live claim true | 1–2 | The page has no equity and no query you still want |
| Comparison / "best X" | One maintained decision page, not a weekly series | 0 unless you will own the cells | You want a Friday listicle habit — go read the comparison spoke instead of cloning it here |

A four-unit month I will actually run for a solo operator:

1. **Week 1 — money.** Rewrite the service or "who we hire for" page.
2. **Week 2 — new spoke.** One unowned buyer question from sales or the prompt panel.
3. **Week 3 — refresh.** The URL that already has impressions or a citation you are about to lose.
4. **Week 4 — new spoke or stop.** Second unowned question *or* take the week to merge duplicates. Stopping is a valid unit if the alternative is thin.

A six-unit month for a two-person team adds a second refresh and one proof page. It does not add three more category explainers.

### What I will not put in the monthly mix

- **A "best X" every week.** One comparison URL you will maintain can stay; a Friday listicle machine cannot. Craft lives in
  [how to write comparison content that AI cites](/blog/how-to-write-comparison-content-that-ai-cites).
- **Four thought-leadership essays.** Opinion without a buyer question is a brand channel, not the citation spine.
- **A spoke for every fan-out variant** of a question you already answered. Google's generative-AI guide flags that as an ineffective long-term strategy and points at scaled content abuse.
- **A 30-post pipeline** as the default for a founder who also delivers the work. That factory is
  [the 30-articles-a-month pipeline](/blog/the-ai-content-pipeline-that-publishes-30-seo-articles-a-month-without-burning-out)
  — ops for teams with editors, not the cadence for a five-page site.

### How I fill the slots without inventing topics

I do not start the month with a theme. I start with a gap list:

- Prompts on the frozen panel where you are missing or only mentioned.
- Questions sales heard twice that no URL owns.
- Money pages that fail the hire test above.
- Existing URLs whose facts you no longer want to sign.

Then I assign each gap a slot. If two gaps are the same primary query, they are one unit. If the list is empty, I do not invent posts to protect the streak.

## When should you stop publishing and fix the five pages you already have?

**Stop adding URLs when the next post would be thinner than the five pages a buyer needs to hire you, or when your prompt panel is still empty on money queries while the blog folder keeps growing.** Fix those five first. New posts after that are optional. New posts instead of that are avoidance.

The five pages I mean are not "your five favorite blogs." They are the minimum set a buyer — or an answer engine building a shortlist — would need:

1. **Who you are** — entity-clear, same name you use everywhere.
2. **What you sell and who it is for** — the money page.
3. **Proof** — a page with dated or hedged receipts, not adjectives.
4. **The one category question you must own** — the spoke that matches your main hire prompt.
5. **How to start** — contact, audit, or package, with enough detail that the page is not a form with no facts.

If those five would embarrass you in a ChatGPT paste, this month is repair. Publishing post 11 is how the site gets noisier and the recommendation stays on a competitor.

| Stop-rule trigger | What I do for 2–4 weeks | What I do not do |
|---|---|---|
| Hire prompts never name you; blog is active | Money-page rewrite + proof page | Another category explainer |
| Two or more URLs share one primary query | Merge, redirect, refresh the survivor | "Differentiate with a new angle" as a fourth URL |
| You cannot name the query for next week's draft | Empty the slot; work the five | Ship a thin placeholder |
| Prompt panel citation rate is flat after a volume sprint | Freeze new posts; refresh the URLs that almost won | Double the publishing rate |
| You are planning pages for every fan-out phrasing | Cut the outline back to owned questions | A programmatic layer you will not edit |
| Facts on the live money pages are wrong | Emergency money-page unit | A scheduled blog about industry news |

### "More pages" is the wrong scoreboard

Page count is easy to report. Citation rate on a frozen panel is not. I have never seen a solo operator get recommended because they crossed 50 blog posts. Google AI Overviews and Google AI Mode can fan a query out and cite a URL that is not the classic #1. That does not mean a pile of thin variants will win the fan-out. Google's own guide says quantity is not quality.

### A two-week repair block I actually assign

When the stop rule fires, change the work order:

1. List the five URLs. A missing slot is the next unit, not a seventh blog idea.
2. Rewrite the money page. Refresh the category question you already tried to own.
3. Re-run last month's prompt panel. Do not add questions to look better.
4. Only then put a new spoke back on the calendar, and only for a gap the panel or sales still shows.

I would rather a 12-URL site with five pages I would cite than a 60-URL site I would not send a buyer. I will not invent a traffic multiple for that preference. I will stand behind the stop rule.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should I publish for AI visibility?

**One or two real units a week for most small sites — and a unit can be a new spoke, a refresh, or a money-page rewrite.** Daily posting is for teams with editors and a duplicate-primary freeze. There is no official ChatGPT or Google AI Overviews frequency. Treat "post N times for citations" as a calendar pitch, not a measurement.

### What topics should I write about to get cited?

**Write the unowned buyer questions that show up in sales and on your frozen prompt panel, plus the money-page facts a hire prompt needs — not a theme list for the month.** Priority is offer clarity, then an unowned question, then a refresh. Skip topics you cannot answer with a receipt or an honest hedge. Phrase the page with the
[question-first model](/blog/the-question-first-content-model-that-gets-you-cited-by-ai);
this FAQ is which topic wins the slot.

### Should I write "best X" listicles every week?

**No. A weekly "best X" habit is how thin pages get on the calendar.** One comparison URL you will maintain can earn citations; a Friday listicle machine usually will not. Craft lives in
[how to write comparison content that AI cites](/blog/how-to-write-comparison-content-that-ai-cites).
On this calendar, comparison is an optional slot, not the default mix.

### Does publishing more pages help Google AI Overviews?

**Not by itself. Google has said a high quantity of pages does not make a site higher quality, and pages spun for every query variant can fall under scaled content abuse.** Ahrefs' March 2026 overlap number is the other half: most AI Overview citations in that sample were **not** also in the first 10 organic results for the same query. Publish a better answer on the URL that should own the question. Measure with Search Console's generative-AI report plus a prompt panel, not page count.

### How do I balance new posts vs refreshing old ones?

**Give refresh an equal or better claim on the week whenever an existing URL already owns the query and the answer aged out; write new only for an unowned question.** Zero refreshes usually means debt. Zero new spokes can be correct while you repair the five pages. Split by equity and gaps, not 50/50 fairness. The rewrite method is
[the refresh framework](/blog/refreshing-old-content-for-the-ai-era-a-practical-framework).

### Is a 30-posts-a-month pipeline the goal for a solo operator?

**No. Thirty posts a month is a factory target for a team with briefs, editors, and QA — not the AI-visibility cadence for a founder who also delivers the work.** The factory write-up is
[the AI content pipeline](/blog/the-ai-content-pipeline-that-publishes-30-seo-articles-a-month-without-burning-out).
Four honest units and a stop rule will beat thirty thin ones. Copying the output number without an edit gate is how scaled content abuse starts.

### Does publishing daily help ChatGPT cite me faster?

**Daily publishing does not make ChatGPT cite you faster if the new URLs are commodity pages or duplicates of a query you already own.** ChatGPT leaned newer than Google AI Overviews in Ahrefs' July 2025 cut, but the average cited URL was still years old, and date-stamping without a change is a known anti-pattern. Ship a better page, then re-run the same prompts.

### How soon after I publish should I check for citations?

**Give it two weeks before you treat a silent panel as a failure; re-run the same frozen prompts, not a new set you invented because you were impatient.** If the panel is still empty after a month and the page is extractable, the gap is usually third-party proof or a money page, not "we needed two more blogs." Log the run the way the
[tracking post](/blog/how-to-track-when-ai-tools-cite-or-recommend-your-business)
describes.
